What this first model does

It begins with a metaphase-II oocyte and fusion-ready sperm already at its membrane. It tracks sperm entry, activation, an idealized effective block, completion of meiosis II, and the normal two-pronucleus endpoint. A second sperm can enter before the block; that branch is flagged as polyspermy.

The oocyte’s chromosomes are still duplicated at the beginning. Distinguishing chromosome sets from DNA content lets us account for the second polar body without losing or inventing DNA.

What comes next

The next measurable milestone is calcium signaling in the egg: cytosol and endoplasmic-reticulum compartments, release, uptake and membrane exchange, with explicit units and conservation checks. Human time-series data and held-out validation are needed before claiming biological prediction.

Later modules will address sperm signaling, energy use, spatial environments and cell division. The long-term ambition is a common cell architecture with distinct cell types and individual states. This increment implements event bookkeeping only.

Biological evidence

The roadmap separates human evidence from other species and from software assumptions. Starting sources include human sperm CatSper electrophysiology, human IZUMO1–JUNO recognition structures, and PLCζ-induced calcium responses in human oocytes. These studies inform future modules; they do not validate this prototype.
