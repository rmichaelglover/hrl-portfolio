Metamusic · a philosophical dialogue

Vibration before hearing

A string vibrating through time. A melody still real when unheard. Noise and melody touching matter differently. An unnamed music, spoken between creatures and beyond speech—simultaneously.

Your foundational claimVibration is a string vibrating over time. Melody is vibration in relation. It remains real when unheard; hearing gives it attention.
Vibration

A time-varying pattern: the string, the field, the changing state.

Melody

A structured relation within vibration, toward which listeners gravitate.

Noise

Another organization of vibration, with its own effects on matter.

Attention

A creature’s response to a pattern that does not depend on that response to exist.

StudentIf no one hears the string, is there still a melody?

GuideIn this axiom system, yes. Vibration exists in time; melody is a real relation within it. Hearing does not summon the pattern into existence. Hearing meets it and gives it attention.

StudentThen music is larger than melody.

GuideYes. Melody is one part of music. There is also noise: vibration that may not organize itself into the forms a listener follows as melody. Both matter. Both can affect matter, in different ways.

StudentMelodious vibration affects matter melodiously; rhythmic, noisy vibration affects it noisily.

GuideThat is the proposed relation. To make a physical model, we would specify what is vibrating, how it couples to a material system, and what measurable change follows. The phrase keeps its larger philosophical force while opening a precise scientific question.

StudentMusic as the language of Adam and Eve; music as the language of You-Know-Who—simultaneously.

GuideOne music speaks between creatures: a shared language before or alongside words. The same music points toward an unnamed source that words cannot contain. The relational and the ineffable sound together.

StudentSo music is inevitable. Melody is logical and intuitive, ineffable and real.

GuideThat is the heart of the metamusic: vibration gives change; relation makes pattern; attention meets the pattern; meaning arrives. The claim is at once a metaphysical axiom and an invitation to build a model.

𝓜 = (V(t), P, N, C, A)

Proposed notation. V is vibration over time; P is melodic structure; N is noise-pattern; C describes coupling to matter; A is organism-relative attention. The notation records the philosophy’s parts. It becomes physics when C and the resulting material changes are operationally defined and tested.

Music is real before it is heard. Hearing is one way the world answers it.

This page develops Michael Emanuel Glover’s philosophical axioms and symbolic proposal. The notation is a conceptual map, not an established physical equation. Its next formal step is to specify a vibrating degree of freedom, a coupling law, and an observable response.
