An exact shortcut for draw claims

After checking whether a claim is already available, an intended move can create a 50-move or threefold claim only if the quiet counter is at least 99, or an exact position in the retained history has already occurred twice. If neither condition holds, testing every legal child cannot discover such a claim.

Necessary-condition gate:
claim already available → keep it
halfmove_clock < 99 AND no twice-seen position → skip intended-claim probes
otherwise → test legal intended moves

Comparing gated and ungated records…

Exact occurrence counts are maintained during make/unmake. The certificate checker deliberately replays claims without the gate, using direct history scans. Mate-priority probing also skips nonchecking moves, since those cannot deliver checkmate.

What the certificate proves

Terminal outcomes are −1, 0 and +1 from White’s perspective. Unknown continuations keep [−1,+1]. White nodes take maxima of the child endpoints; Black nodes take minima. Unvisited legal replies retain their full unknown intervals.

A White-winning witness can reach the global +1 cap; a Black-winning witness can reach −1. Those exact bounds justify stopping that local search. An ordering score alone never discards a legal move. Optional draw claims enter as zero-valued actions.

The replay checker validates legal edges, complete root history, endings and interval propagation. Removing a required defensive reply weakens the recomputed bound and invalidates a claimed win. It shares the legal-move library with search but does not use search rankings.

Exact roots return an outcome-optimal move or draw-claim action. Unresolved roots return no certified move. Mate distance is not optimized.
