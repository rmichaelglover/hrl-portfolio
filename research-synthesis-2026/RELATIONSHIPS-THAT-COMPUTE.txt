Abstract
I have built a research programme around a simple computational proposition: an object becomes
more intelligible when its possible identities are evaluated in relationship with other objects, at
the appropriate scale, while an unresolved answer remains available. The HRL portfolio now
contains reusable inference code, exact representation results, a convergence-guaranteed
alternative update, controlled higher-order interaction experiments, measured-data applications,
bounded game proofs, and a working ROS 2 simulation. These are different kinds of achievement,
and this paper reports them in their proper units.

The strongest engineering result is an exact sparse implementation of a structured signed
relaxation update. A recorded 100,000-node path uses 19,999,168 operator bytes, against
approximately 720 billion bytes for the hypothetical dense signed kernel. At 1,024 nodes the
sparse implementation is 27.81 times faster in the recorded environment. A separate ternary
study provides an exact two-coordinate description of uncertainty, a 12.2-fold local speedup, and
a contractive alternative that converged in all 1,536 tested binary/ternary runs. A chirality audit
establishes that the signed cyclic factor used in an earlier demonstration is reducible to oriented
pair terms, while a thresholded positive-only predicate retains a genuine higher-order component.

The programme also includes synthetic interaction discovery, reciprocal grounding, spreadsheet
auditing, morphology, hydrological routing, and quantum-state representation. Interaction
discovery recovers all five generating supports in 20 independent-input seeds, attaining 97.90%
held-out explained variance with 14 fitted parameters; its no-relaxation ablation performs
identically, and hidden interactions expose a substantial blind spot. Reciprocal grounding
achieves 328 correct matches among 332 accepted matches, with 41.50% coverage, but fails
completely under a shared wrong dictionary. Recursive representation refinement extends this
work without making agreement an external truth criterion.

Measured-data studies include 23-subject wearable posture classification, leakage-audited
CRISPR ranking, public threat-report entity extraction, economic forecasting, descriptive
economic series, and a conditional Milky Way mass-model comparison. Their results vary: some
are useful improvements or close fits; some reveal stronger simple baselines; none warrants a
universal HRL superiority claim. Chess work now separates heuristic preference, bounded proof,
and physical execution. The ROS apparatus has completed full numerical games and exposes

1. The research claim I am prepared to make
I do not want this work reduced to a collection of attractive demonstrations. The demonstrations
are interfaces to algorithms, numerical experiments, formal questions, and a growing evidence
record. Some are early sketches. Others contain exact identities, executed benchmarks, or
replayable certificates. A fair account must recognize that difference without making the early
work disappear.

My central contribution is the construction and testing of a relational research architecture
across several representations and domains. A measured point can receive a marker identity; an
observation can receive a posture label; a candidate move can receive a tactical role; a relation
can become an object at the next level. The architecture asks not only which answer has the
largest local score, but which assignments support one another, what evidence anchors them, and
where the system should refrain from deciding.

The common language does not make all implementations identical. The public dense engine,
structured sparse signed engine, generic factor engine, recursive GRRLE substrate, reciprocal
graph update, and interaction-discovery learner have different contracts. This paper treats them
as related members of a programme. It does not smuggle an equivalence claim into the acronym
HRL.

The evidence supports contributions in implementation, representation, verification, experimental
design, and integration. Establishing priority over the full literature would require a separate
systematic review. I can report original portfolio constructions and their measured behavior
without declaring every ingredient unprecedented. Rosenfeld, Hummel, Zucker, and the many
researchers whose algorithms and datasets I use remain part of the account. [S01–S06]

This synthesis covers the working portfolio repository, the relaxation-publication study, the
triadic-unity study, selected original relaxation-labeling modules, the ROS workspace, and several
local research directories. It includes relevant local artifacts even when a publication-facing page
is absent or incomplete. It is not a census of every file on the machine, every earlier conversation,
or inaccessible material. Duplicate source snapshots are provenance, not additional independent
experiments.

3. The engine: assignments supported by context
The public core stores a nonnegative strength vector for each object. Let p(i,a) denote the
strength of label a for object i, and let C(i,a,j,b) describe how the assignment of label b to object j
supports or inhibits label a at object i. Pairwise support is the sum of C(i,a,j,b)p(j,b) over
neighboring assignments. The dense reference stores a four-index compatibility tensor. Its clarity
makes it useful as a reference, but its storage grows quadratically in the number of objects and
labels.

The persistent-prior update uses a multiplicative base proportional to prior(i,a)^s times
p(i,a)^(1−s). A scaled support term modifies that base, and each row is normalized. At s=0 the
prior seeds the initial state; for positive s it continues to influence subsequent updates. The code
provides a separate noise label and retains iteration histories when requested.

This is an inspectable implementation choice, not a declaration that every resulting strength is a
Bayesian posterior. A normalized score is not automatically a calibrated probability, and repeated
reinforcement of the same evidence is not repeated independent observation. Likewise, numerical
convergence means that the selected update has stopped changing within tolerance. It does not
mean the assignments are correct.

The practical value is that a caller can inspect the prior, compatibility, trajectory, unresolved
assignments, and stopping condition. The mathematical value is that a precise reference update
makes exact reformulations and ablations possible. The architectural value is reuse: the same
object/label contract can serve different domains while the evidential meaning of each domain
remains explicit. [S01]

4. Persistent priors, null labels, and honest uncertainty
Two recurring problems motivated the public implementation. First, an initialization-only prior
can lose its influence as contextual reinforcement accumulates. Second, a closed label vocabulary
can force an outlier into an inappropriate category. Persistent priors and an explicit null option
address these problems in the specified model.

The null label is more than a cosmetic extra color. It gives the system a place to put evidence that
the current vocabulary cannot adequately explain. Ghost detections in a tracking sequence,
unmatched objects in a grounding task, and unresolved interpretations in a language prototype
require different domain semantics, but all benefit from not forcing a false commitment.

There is no claim that all uncertainty is the same. A ternary unresolved weight, a null assignment,
a tied prediction, a budget-exhausted proof search, an out-of-distribution observation, and an
interval that still spans several outcomes are different states. Their shared lesson is procedural:
retain the reason for uncertainty instead of compressing every non-answer into a confident label.

Hard zero priors can also matter. Under a multiplicative rule they can prevent an assignment
from becoming positive. That may implement an intended anchor or accidentally prevent recovery
from a bad assumption. The author of a compatibility model must therefore declare whether zero
means impossible, merely unlikely, or missing. A small positive floor and an exact zero have
different consequences.

I regard these distinctions as research progress because they make the system's failures legible.
The portfolio's later audits show that a null rule can itself have unwanted global dependence.
Uncertainty handling must be tested as carefully as the ordinary labels. [S01–S03, S07]


5. Exact ternary coordinates: what ISH adds
The triadic-unity study gives a clean answer to a question that initially arrived in philosophical
language: what does an unresolved third state add? For a normalized state (f,u,t), define signed
support m=t−f and unresolved mass u. Then f=(1−u−m)/2 and t=(1−u+m)/2, with 0≤u≤1 and
|m|≤1−u. The three weights therefore have two independent coordinates. Keeping m alone loses
information.

For example, a state split equally between false and true and a state fully unresolved both have
m=0. They differ completely in u. A binary summary of signed support cannot distinguish
balanced opposition from an explicit unresolved state. This is an exact representation fact, rather
than evidence that the universe possesses a privileged ternary ontology.

For the study's positive-neighbor kernel, the raw supports reduce to (d−M)/2, (d+U)/2, and
(d+M)/2. Here d is total neighbor weight, and M and U are weighted sums of the neighbors'
signed support and unresolved mass. ISH has greatest raw support when U≥|M|. This makes the
previously informal notion of unresolved relational evidence computationally explicit.

The independent two-coordinate implementation matched the three-weight implementation
within 3.33×10⁻¹⁶ across the initial study. The result applies to the stated structured kernel. It
does not prove that every three-label model admits the same update reduction or that adding an

6. Sparse support: a demonstrated scaling result
The signed publication study supplies one of the clearest accomplishments in the portfolio. A
structured signed relaxation update was represented exactly through sparse local operators
rather than a dense four-index kernel. Across random seeds, symmetric and asymmetric graphs,
mixing settings, and noise controls, the sparse trajectories matched the dense reference to
floating-point tolerance. Ten public fixtures reproduced with maximum discrepancy 1.11×10⁻¹⁶.

At 1,024 objects, the recorded median times were 0.1805 seconds for the dense reference, 0.0418
seconds for the factorized-dense formulation, and 0.00649 seconds for sparse support. The sparse
speedup against the dense reference was 27.81-fold. Compatibility storage fell from 75,497,472
bytes to 203,848 operator bytes.

At 100,000 objects on a path, the sparse run used 799,965 nonzeros, 19,999,168 operator bytes,
and 0.599 seconds in the recorded environment. The corresponding dense signed kernel would
require approximately 720 GB in decimal units. That dense allocation is a calculated comparison,
not an allocation successfully executed on the machine. The sparse byte count describes
operators, not total peak process memory.

A separate positive-kernel ternary benchmark at 512 objects and 100 updates measured 8.05
milliseconds sparse versus 97.83 milliseconds dense, a 12.2-fold speedup, with maximum
discrepancy 5.55×10⁻¹⁶. These are separate studies and should not be pooled into one headline
speedup.

The contribution is precise: for specified structured compatibility, the implementation preserves
the intended update while changing its computational feasibility. It is not a theorem that every
dense compatibility tensor can be compressed to local sparse factors. [S02, S03]

7. Locality audit: isolated objects should not rewrite an
active component
The publication study found a subtle algorithmic issue in the legacy noise semantics. The original
noise contribution samples incompatibility over the global state length. Appending isolated
objects can therefore change the active component's probabilities even when no new relation
connects the components.

In the recorded padding experiment, adding 32, 128, and 512 isolated objects changed
active-node probabilities by mean amounts 0.0373, 0.0561, and 0.0642. The corrected local mode
produced exactly zero change in the tested runs. That is a testable locality property: unrelated
padding should not alter a component whose support is defined locally.

The distinction between an exact optimization and a semantic correction matters here. Sparse
legacy mode can reproduce the old global behavior exactly. Local mode intentionally changes the
model to repair the unwanted dependence. Calling both operations an optimization would conceal
a substantive change in what the algorithm means.

This result has consequences beyond one implementation. A system that adds more observed
objects, map cells, candidate words, or recursive structures should know whether its
normalization changes existing evidence. Otherwise scale itself becomes a hidden input. Locality
tests should accompany sparse reformulations and null-label designs.

I count the discovery and repair as a contribution. The research programme improved by finding
a counterexample to its own assumptions and producing a controlled alternative. Preserving the
old reference makes the repair auditable rather than silently rewriting history. [S02]


8. Convergence: a guaranteed alternative with explicit
assumptions
The original row-minmax normalization can amplify very small support differences. The triadic
study found 48 nonconverged binary conditions among 768 follow-up conditions even after 1,000
iterations. Their prediction scores are finite-iteration outputs. A plot that appears settled and a
small last-step change do not supply a theorem for all inputs.

The study therefore implemented a separate prior-respecting alternative of the form
p_next=softmax(log(prior)+β·normalized_neighbor_support). Under the stated
assumptions—nonnegative graph weights with row sums at most one, bounded similarities in
[0,1], and β<1—the update is contractive in the maximum per-object L1 metric. A contraction
supplies a unique fixed point and convergence from every admissible initial state. Zero-prior
handling must respect the proof's support conventions.

With β=0.8, all 1,536 binary and ternary runs in the follow-up converged. A residual-based bound
translates the size of an update into a bound on distance from the fixed point under the
contraction constant. This is stronger than merely choosing an iteration cap.

The guarantee belongs to the modified update, not to the whole portfolio. Its prediction accuracy

Stability and predictive usefulness are distinct objectives; a stable method can converge reliably
to a poor answer.

The practical next step is to make update families and their assumptions explicit in the engine
interface. A caller should be able to choose exact legacy equivalence, local corrected semantics,
or a convergent alternative, and understand what changes with that choice. [S03]


9. Higher-order structure: the chirality claim after audit
The portfolio's earlier chirality demonstration claimed a sharp contrast between pairwise
compatibility and a triangular interaction. The audit made the claim more accurate. Exhaustive
enumeration showed that the particular signed cyclic score is exactly decomposable into oriented
pair terms. An undirected symmetric pair model cannot represent the relevant orientation; an
oriented pair model can.

On 77 disjoint triangles over 40 seeds, the symmetric pair rule averaged 2.05 positive, 1.95
negative, and 73 degenerate outcomes. Exact three-body and oriented-pair rules averaged 74.65
positive, 0.90 negative, and 1.45 degenerate outcomes. Their trajectories agreed with maximum
error 1.62×10⁻¹². This is a result about the information retained by a representation, not a
universal victory of triangles over edges.

A hard positive-only predicate remained different from the pairwise signed score, with reported
RMS discrepancy 0.157 and a genuine higher-order residual. The relevant distinction is therefore
between a decomposable score and an irreducible factor under a specified label representation
and allowed pair family. Merely drawing a triangle does not prove irreducible three-body
computation.

This correction strengthens the programme. Orientation, interaction order, auxiliary variables,
and the permitted representation must be specified before comparing expressive power. A
pairwise model augmented with new state variables is not the same comparator as a symmetric
pair model over the original labels.

I retain the ambition to study higher-order relational structure. I also retain the audit that
prevents an attractive example from carrying a theorem it does not establish. The next
benchmarks should deliberately include both pair-decomposable and irreducible factors. [S02,
S04]


10. GRRLE and Simplicial Relax: recursion made
executable
Simplicial Relax names the broader architecture: objects and label fields, pair and higher-order
factors, respected evidence, an unresolved option, and inspectable interfaces. The generic factor
engine stores explicit sparse rules rather than the full dense compatibility tensor. GRRLE Strong
adds finite recursive structures, dual roles, promotion, and perturbation diagnostics.

The inspected GRRLE Strong release contains Python and header-only C++ implementations. Its
original Python demonstration constructed 20 nodes across depths −2 through 2; the C++
demonstration compiled and ran. A repair made the Python extra-depth argument control actual
repeated promotion passes. Structural, boundedness, duality, depth, and input checks accompany
the exhibit.

These achievements establish an executable substrate. They do not make the Python and C++
implementations numerically interchangeable. Their random generators, defaults, and updates
differ. Cross-language equivalence needs serialized fixtures that fix initial trees, relation values,
update schedules, and observables.

Projection is another important boundary. A default demonstration quickly reaches a projection
boundary; thereafter its output changes little. That is a property of the selected projected
dynamics. It should not be mistaken for discovery of a uniquely correct interpretation.

The architecture's useful promise is that relations can become objects in a finite next-level
problem, while the entire computation remains inspectable. Whether a given promotion rule
improves generalization is an empirical question. The interaction-discovery and
recursive-grounding studies begin to answer it under controlled conditions rather than relying on
the presence of a hierarchy as evidence of benefit. [S04–S07]


11. Meta-renormalization I: representation can dominate
an additive summary
Experiment 001 began from a synthetic equation containing specified pair interactions and a
fourth-order term. Its recovered source uses eight independent binary components, 12,000
observations, a 9,000/3,000 train/test split, and noise standard deviation 0.3. Reproduction gave
MSE values 4.75248, 4.74778, and 0.08968 for the three reported representations.

The strongest model was given features matched to the generating interactions. That is a
meaningful demonstration of representational adequacy. An additive or lossy coarse summary
cannot generally retain information that an interaction-dependent target requires. But supplying
the right interactions is not autonomous discovery.

The reproduction also corrected a metric label. The original script reported R-squared values of
approximately 2.281%, 2.378%, and 98.156%, while an earlier summary called them explained
variance. These metrics are related but different: explained variance removes the mean residual
before taking its variance, while R-squared uses mean squared error. The new artifact stores
both.

This is the right role for an initial proof of concept. It exposes a question worth pursuing: can the
system discover the useful interaction supports without receiving them? The first experiment
does not answer that question, but it makes clear why the question matters.

I also distinguish the portfolio's name meta-renormalization from a demonstrated physical
renormalization-group method. Fixed pair averages are a deliberately lossy comparator, not a full
implementation of the established physical theory. The research here concerns representation,

12. Meta-renormalization II: autonomous selection
within a finite grammar
Experiment 002 removes the identities of the generating supports from the learner. Each seed
generates 12,000 observations of eight Gaussian components. Four randomly chosen disjoint
pairs contribute to the target, and two pairs jointly contribute a fourth-order term. Linear
contributions and independent Gaussian noise remain present.

The protocol uses 6,000 fitting observations, 3,000 structure-selection observations, and 3,000
sealed test observations. All 28 unordered pairs are candidates. Selected disjoint pairs can
propose canonical four-component products. Equivalent parenthesizations are deduplicated. The
hidden supports are used for evaluation, not passed to the learner.

Candidate evidence comes from validation MSE changes after fitting or removing a term.
Active/inactive strengths receive damped relational updates. Positive-gain and strength gates
control promotion. The grammar, gain calculation, gates, and compatibility weights are designed.
The learned part is which candidates are selected.

Across 20 independent-input seeds, the hierarchy recovered all four pair supports and the
fourth-order support in every seed. It achieved 97.90% mean held-out explained variance and
MSE 0.09076, using 14 fitted parameters. The exhaustive degree-four multilinear baseline
achieved 97.86%, MSE 0.09237, and 163 fitted parameters. The additive model achieved 3.85%
and MSE 4.25602.

This is a substantial controlled result in compact structure discovery. The correct parameter
comparison is fitted model size, not total computational cost: the hierarchy also spends work
proposing and evaluating candidates. Selection repeatedly queries validation data, so its selection
score is not an untouched generalization estimate. The final test remains separate. [S08]

13. What the discovery failures teach
The no-relaxation ablation produced the same predictions and selected structure in the
independent-input principal condition. Therefore the observed gain belongs to the whole
representation-and-selection procedure; it cannot be attributed specifically to the recurrent
relaxation step. Including an iterative update does not show that the update earned its cost.

The pure hidden fourth-order condition is more revealing. When useful pair-level parents are
absent, the hierarchy does not propose the hidden interaction. Mean explained variance falls to
17.20%, while exhaustive enumeration achieves 91.28%. With correlated inputs, the hierarchy
reaches 81.35% against 99.08% for the exhaustive baseline, and generating-support recall falls to
0.70.

These failures identify an architectural restriction: a parent-first grammar can miss interactions
whose lower-level components have little predictive evidence individually. Correlation also makes
greedy commitments ambiguous. The current learner does not prune selected terms or revisit the
full structure globally.

There is an identifiability limit even with perfect prediction. Products such as (x1x2)(x3x4) and
(x1x3)(x2x4) define the same four-component monomial. Recovering the support set does not
recover a unique latent tree or establish causality.

My next claim should therefore be earned by stronger comparisons: sparse polynomial
regression, hierarchical interaction selection, beam search, and equation-family holdouts.
Candidate generation should sometimes leap over unsupported intermediate levels. The paper's
contribution is not that recursion always wins; it is that we now know a concrete regime where
compact discovery succeeds and concrete regimes where the promotion rule fails. [S08]

14. Reciprocal grounding: things label words, words
label things
Reciprocal grounding operationalizes one of my central intuitions: exchange the roles of objects
and labels, and inspect where differently informed assignments support one another. The first
experiment is deliberately narrow. Sixteen word identifiers and sixteen matching thing instances
share a hidden correspondence, with four unmatched things. These are synthetic feature vectors,
not a general model of language meaning.

Each direction receives its own evidence and relation graphs. The graph-factorized support is
A·P_real·Bᵀ. A damped softmax update keeps the original logits present at every step and retains
an unknown column. Mutual unique winners, directional strength thresholds, margins, and
perturbation checks define acceptance.

On held-out independent-view seeds, reciprocal acceptance produced 328 correct matches among
332 accepted, or 98.80% accepted accuracy at 41.50% coverage. Forward-only selection
produced 381 correct among 401, or 95.01% at 50.12% coverage. Thresholds were selected on
separate seeds and frozen before the held-out conditions.

These are different operating points. Higher accuracy among fewer accepted cases does not
automatically establish a better system. The source publishes risk–coverage curves and
whole-seed bootstrap intervals, rather than treating all observations as independent.

The implementation also offers an audit interface with provisional, rejected, and revoked records.
Its illustrative oracle is explicitly a simulator oracle, not an available real-world verifier. Bounded
anchor bonuses can be removed on a fresh rebuild after revocation. This establishes an auditable
mechanism for provisional assignments; it does not establish that agreement creates truth. [S07]


15. Agreement can be completely wrong
The reciprocal study contains an especially important counterexample. Shift both dictionaries by
the same wrong permutation, and both directions can agree confidently while naming the wrong
things. Under that condition, the reciprocal rule accepted 338 pairs and got zero correct. The
internal consistency tests did not rescue correctness.

A copied-reverse baseline is also strong. It adds no independent evidence, yet achieves 423
correct matches among 431 accepts, or 98.14% at 53.87% coverage in the independent-view
condition. Mutual uniqueness can filter assignments even when the second view is derived from
the first. The filtering effect must not be reported as independent corroboration.

With correlated noise, reciprocal accepted accuracy was 95.76% at 50.12% coverage. With an
uninformative reverse view, it retained only eight accepts, seven correct, or 1.00% coverage. That
small surviving sample cannot carry a broad grounding claim.

30. Rolling chess: physical execution as an additional
constraint system
The rolling-chess apparatus connects a legal symbolic game to a two-axis wooden maze. A
replenishable metal sphere represents a move attempt. Its source is the selected piece's square;
open destination wells correspond to legal landing squares. Entering a well commits a legal move
rather than disposing of the mover's chess material.

The implemented board is 0.8 metres square, with 0.1-metre squares, sphere radius 0.012
metres, and hole radius 0.034 metres. Fixed substeps are 1/240 second. For a uniform solid
sphere under no-slip rolling, the acceleration factor is 5/7 of the tangential gravitational
acceleration. The model adds illustrative resistance, damping, contact response, a speed cap, and
bounded roll/pitch.

This is a quasi-static rigid apparatus with absorbing wells. It omits slipping, actuator inertia, full
angular contact, calibrated wood/metal friction, and detailed high-speed rim collisions. The
chess-piece shapes are visual markers; wood and stranded spheres are physical obstacles.

The initial-position observation experiment narrows compatible legal transitions from 20 to two
when the source e2 is known, and from two to one when entry at e4 is observed. That is
state-transition reconstruction from evidence. The controller found physical witnesses for all 20
opening moves, so the supplied maze establishes no additional opening-move exclusion.

A failed route remains unresolved. It is not a proof that no continuous trajectory exists, nor a
reason to remove an orthodox legal move. Physical exclusion would require a sound
apparatus-specific certificate and a clear declaration of whether the claim concerns ordinary
chess or a new physical variant. [S30]

33. Anchored geometry: information links and
convergence certificates
Hyperspace develops physical–informational geometry as a modeling proposal and proves results
for specified finite averaging systems. In an anchored graph, free-node states can be written
x_free(next)=Qx_free+Rx_anchor. When every free node reaches an anchor under the stated
transition assumptions, the free block is transient, and the equilibrium is determined by
(I−Q)⁻¹Rx_anchor.

This supports a useful interpretation of multiple anchors: each free node receives a mixture of
anchor values weighted by hitting probabilities. The same system connects to a Dirichlet-energy
formulation under the appropriate symmetry assumptions. These are established mathematical
families applied and worked through in the portfolio, not discoveries of new physical dimensions.

In the 40-node synthetic geographic cycle, recorded spectral radius was approximately 0.998459
and the largest expected absorption time was 800 steps. Adding twelve information links changed
these to approximately 0.996393 and 326.78. The shortcut run crossed error 10⁻³ at update
1,964; the original cycle had not crossed within 4,000 updates.

The work also includes a harmonic residual of 1.11×10⁻¹⁶ on a triangular lattice, exact
small-chain calculations, first-order sensitivity, and a stopping certificate under its contraction
conditions. These results make the phrase all roads lead home mathematically specific:
reachability, weight conditions, and anchors do the work.

The counterexamples matter. Periodic systems and unsuitable decaying weights can invalidate
naive convergence intuitions. Adding a link is not universally beneficial under every normalization
or objective. The contribution is a documented finite model, worked derivations, and reproducible
tests of when informational connections accelerate anchored agreement. [S33]

The closure work became more rigorous by separating descriptive slots from physical degrees of
freedom and by placing gravitational and electromagnetic residuals in typed spaces. A direct-sum
residual can require both sectors to vanish without adding incompatible quantities as though
their units and domains were identical.

One exact finite construction is F_P(u,v)=(u+v,v+Pu). Its kernel satisfies v=−u and Pu=u. The
nullity is the number of cycles in the permutation P. For six-component u, identity, a
transposition, and a six-cycle give nullities six, five, and one while the descriptive slot count
remains fixed. This demonstrates bridge-controlled compatible modes.

Another conditional result says that a continuous multiplicative map from additive action to U(1)
has exponential phase form. The assumptions already specify the phase group and composition.
The result does not derive the Born rule, Planck's constant, or quantum theory from closure.

The paired-real control is essential. A complex operator A+iB can be represented by the real
block matrix with rows (A,−B) and (B,A). Complex propagation therefore has an equivalent

summed kernel; reversible norm preservation needs its own condition.

The finite mesh implementation verifies gauge invariance and refinement for a flat-background

35. Quantum-state representation: large possibility
spaces can have small descriptions
Magnum Quantreleviathone studies a specific family of entangled states on a classical computer.
Starting from zero qubits, a Hadamard gate on the first qubit followed by controlled-NOT gates
produces the GHZ state: two basis branches with equal amplitudes. An induction supplies the
exact family-level result.

The code checked dense simulations for every qubit count from two through twenty—nineteen
circuits. All final amplitudes matched the expected floating-point values in the implementation,
with normalization error at rounding scale. At twenty qubits, dense complex128 amplitude
storage is 16,777,216 bytes. The tested sparse payload contains two amplitudes and two unsigned
indices, totaling 48 bytes.

That payload comparison excludes Python object overhead, metadata, temporary arrays, and
executable memory. It is not a claim that the complete program uses 48 bytes, and it is not a
timing demonstration of quantum advantage. The sparse index convention also has a finite tested
range.

36. Astronomy: from a shell analogy to a measured-data
comparison
The Oort–halo inquiry began with spherical Newtonian models. Equal total mass in a hollow shell
and an extended cored halo produces different interior forces. A spherical shell contributes zero
net acceleration in its cavity but a nonzero constant potential relative to infinity. The extended
halo contains mass inside the tracer radius and can contribute substantial local acceleration.
Geometry alone does not identify composition.

The next study uses all 38 numerical rows of Eilers and colleagues' published Milky Way rotation
table and a specified flattened baryonic potential based on McMillan's model. A two-parameter
NFW halo is fitted to the 30 bins below 20 kpc, with a declared illustrative weighting floor. Disk
force is computed from the flattened potential; speeds are combined in quadrature, not added
linearly.

The fixed baryonic model has RMS speed residual 66.72 km/s over the fitting interval. Adding the
fitted halo lowers it to approximately 2.00 km/s. The eight excluded outer bins have RMS residual
15.30 km/s. The fit therefore does not precisely explain the entire radial range.

Changing baryonic mass normalization from 0.8 to 1.2 moves the fitted halo scale radius from
7.83 to 27.13 kpc while retaining small fitting-interval residuals. This is a sensitivity range, not a
confidence interval. The data are inferred circular speeds under dynamical assumptions, and the
baryonic baseline has its own inherited modeling constraints.

The contribution is a reproducible conditional mass-model comparison with source extraction,
numerical-force checks, and a visible degeneracy. It neither identifies dark-matter particles nor
eliminates every possible alternative mass distribution or gravitational model. [S38, S39]

40. Project Genesis: observational structure and
constructed dynamics
The Genesis series deserves a place in the results record because it brought external scientific

The coordinate construction uses a linear Hubble-distance approximation and a display wedge

agreement with established web classifiers under controlled geometry. [S47]

41. What has been contributed across the programme
I can now identify a set of contributions that survives a demanding reading. First, exact sparse
formulations make structured relational dynamics feasible at scales where the dense reference is
prohibitive. Second, ternary coordinates and locality audits clarify what uncertainty represents
and how normalization can change its meaning. Third, convergence is available for a stated
alternative rather than asserted for every update.

Fourth, the higher-order work distinguishes orientation loss from genuinely irreducible
interaction. Fifth, recursive interaction discovery and grounding have become executable
experiments with sealed tests, ablations, and explicit failure conditions. Sixth, the applied work
has produced real-data evaluations whose limits are inspectable rather than hidden behind a
gallery image.

Seventh, chess research now has a separation between preference and proof, including
replayable bounded certificates and history-sensitive draw semantics. Eighth, ROS integration
connects beliefs, authority, symbolic decisions, and physical execution in a working virtual
apparatus. Ninth, the physics-facing programme contains correct finite results and controlled
numerical constructions while retaining a clear boundary around untested physical hypotheses.

These contributions form a research platform. They do not add up by summing incompatible
percentages, multiplying benchmark speedups, or treating repeated copies of a dataset as new
evidence. Their coherence comes from a common method: represent relationships, retain
uncertainty, expose the computation, and let a counterexample change the claim.

I intend the next stage to be more demanding, not more timid. The work is ready for focused
external scrutiny: independent reruns, stronger baselines, real task families, and explicit tests of
which relational mechanisms improve decisions. The accomplishments already made provide the
means to conduct that scrutiny. [S01–S50]

42. The next experiments that can change the
conclusion
For the engine, compare update families under identical compatibility, data, stopping, and
memory conventions. Include construction cost and peak memory. Test adversarial priors,
disconnected components, oscillatory cases, and high label counts. Calibrated prediction,
numerical stability, and selective risk should be measured separately.

For hierarchy, hold out equation families and vary degree, dimension, correlation, and noise.
Compare sparse polynomial and hierarchical interaction methods. Permit proposals that do not
depend on successful lower-order parents. A relaxation-specific gain should survive a matched
no-relaxation comparator.

For grounding and language, move to measured referring expressions or sensor observations,
with many-to-many relations, unknown objects, correlated evidence, and stronger assignment
baselines. Audit anchors with genuinely separate evidence. Evaluate fresh identities as well as
fresh observations of existing identities.

For chess, freeze a position suite, compare shared search with different ordering policies, and
verify every claimed strategy. Report solved fraction, node count, wall time, proof size, and
budget exhaustion. For physical chess, add sound reachability enclosures before claiming
exclusion. For robotics, measure sensor and speech performance independently of permission
enforcement.

For astronomy and closure, define observables before fitting. Preserve non-flat geometry, units,
gauge structure, and refinement controls where they are relevant. Compare equivalent
representations with matched resources. In biomedical work, strengthen assay generalization and
model identification before escalating to organism-level interpretations.

The decisive question is not whether the programme can generate another impressive display. It
can. The decisive question is which additional evidence would make a skeptical expert change a
particular conclusion. Each next experiment should be designed around that question.

Appendix C. An operational vocabulary for interpreting
the portfolio
Strength is a numerical state used by an update rule. Normalization alone does not make it a
calibrated probability. Support is the contribution of other assignments or evidence under an
explicit compatibility rule. Confidence is often a winning strength or margin in these
implementations; its empirical reliability must be measured separately.

Prior is input preference or evidence that precedes the current relational update. It may be
heuristic, learned, measured, or fixed by a domain rule. The word does not guarantee a complete
Bayesian generative model. Anchor is an assignment deliberately held or biased by stronger
evidence. Reusing an anchor across views couples their errors.

ISH is an unresolved ternary component in specified models. Null can mean no available label
explains an object. Abstention is a decision not to return an ordinary answer. Conflict can mean
both positive and negative derivations are present. These states need explicit semantics rather
than one universal interpretation.

Hierarchy means that representations or constraints are organized across declared levels.
Recursion means that an operation revisits or constructs related structures through a finite
process in the executed systems. Neither word proves infinite depth, improved accuracy, or
causal identification.

Convergence is a statement about a sequence under a rule and a metric. Correctness concerns
whether the output meets its contract or matches a specified reference. Truth about an external
phenomenon requires the corresponding external evidence. The three should not share one
dashboard light.

Certificate is an object a verifier can check against a defined claim. Witness may establish that
one route or continuation exists. Exclusion requires ruling out all relevant alternatives. A failed
search, sampled rollout, or exhausted budget supplies neither an exclusion certificate nor a draw
theorem by itself.

Closure names a specified residual or composition condition. A small closure residual is not
automatically a good prediction, a causal identification result, or a physical unification. Mass,
informational weight, and surprisal likewise belong to different mathematical and physical
contracts unless a bridge is explicitly defined and tested.
