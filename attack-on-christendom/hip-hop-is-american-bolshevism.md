# Hip-Hop Is American Bolshevism

*Attack on Christendom · A Polemical Essay · October 9, 2026*

*Kanye as Lenin without the bloodshed; Michael Emanuel Glover as Trotsky without the bloodshed.*

Kanye West is our reason for writing. Here is an artist through whom we can ask whether the person expected to provide entertainment may instead announce a claim upon the world. The microphone becomes a pulpit, the studio a workshop, and the finished work a refusal to remain somebody else’s cheap raw material.

I write as a Marxist and an economic libertarian, intentionally holding those commitments in tension. This essay puts the Marxist side forward: labour creates; ownership collects; institutions instruct the creator to be grateful for admission. My libertarian suspicion of concentrated authority remains present, particularly when the revolutionary proposes becoming the next unquestionable authority. That suspicion belongs in the revolution itself.

## The messenger is human

Let Kanye enter this essay as a nondivine messianic figure: a human herald, accountable and capable of failure, who brings an announcement that the poor possess voices worth hearing and powers worth developing. He does not become Christ. He takes a place among people who must answer to the demands of the good news.

The scriptural measure is severe. Luke 4:18 announces good news to the poor; Matthew 6:24 declares that God and mammon cannot share a servant. These are tests for our messenger as well as for the institutions he confronts. A luxury price tag cannot settle either examination. [Luke 4:18 and Matthew 6:24, KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%204%3A18%3BMatthew%206%3A24&version=KJV).

The stronger man in this argument outcompetes, outworks, outsings, outraps, outthinks, and outwrites the worshippers of mammon. This is the ambition we place before him, not a statistical finding that he has already surpassed every rival. Strength means making work the gatekeepers cannot honestly dismiss, then widening the gate for other workers. If victory ends with one famous man being admitted to the counting-house, the poor have gained a celebrity and the counting-house has gained an advertisement.

## Lenin without the bloodshed

“Kanye is Vladimir Lenin minus the bloodshed” is our deliberately excessive literary proposition. It takes from the comparison a demand to reorganize who may speak, create, and direct production. It refuses killing, terror, coercive rule, and the supposition that people can be sacrificed to an idea. Those exclusions transform the analogy. This is a cultural revolution whose instruments are work, argument, association, and art.

And I am Trotsky minus the bloodshed: the writer insisting that a revolution must not settle into a comfortable new management. That is my chosen role within this polemic, not a claim to historical rank. The permanent demand is that the promise reach the people whose labour made the platform possible. If the movement acquires a throne, the critic must keep his pen.

A revolution that can tolerate only praise has already mistaken its publicity department for its conscience.

## The voice of the voiceless

Hip-hop is American Bolshevism in the sense proposed here: an insurgency of expression against the distribution of permission. The people described as voiceless have voices. What they lack is the microphone, the room, the distribution contract, the presumption that their account of life belongs in the public record.

The studio offers a means of production that can turn testimony into a public force. Rhythm can make an account memorable; a verse can carry an accusation further than its author could walk. The person dismissed as a social problem becomes the person explaining the society that dismissed him.

This reading gives particular attention to Black American experience without pretending that Black Americans share one politics or one biography. Christian and Muslim voices, secular voices, people who have left gangs, and artists performing gangster personae cannot be collapsed into a single identity. A staged outlaw is not automatically a witness to criminal life; a former offender is not permanently reducible to an offence. The question is what the work says, what it makes possible, and whom it serves.

Nor is Marxism the drug trade. An illegal market can reproduce exploitation with terrible efficiency. Fear is not solidarity; domination is not emancipation. The reformed gangster matters to this essay because reform can refuse both the conditions that narrowed a life and the injuries inflicted within that life. The voice grows stronger when it can tell the truth about its own conduct.

## Mammon can buy a revolutionary costume

The industry can sell revolt as readily as obedience. It can make poverty into scenery, danger into a marketing category, and a person’s escape into proof that everyone left behind simply failed to hustle hard enough. The Marxist objection asks who owns the recording, who receives the proceeds, and whose work disappears behind the star’s name.

Kanye is compelling precisely because the proposed messenger stands inside that contradiction. Commercial achievement supplies reach and bargaining power; it also makes the temptation to confuse freedom with personal wealth unusually strong. Our essay demands that he outthink the worshippers of mammon, including the worshipper who might appear in his own mirror.

## No exemption for the herald

Admiration cannot exempt Kanye from responsibility for antisemitism. Adidas ended its partnership with Ye in October 2022, citing his antisemitic comments and conduct. That episode belongs in any honest account of the figure we have chosen. [Adidas statement, October 25, 2022](https://www.adidas-group.com/en/media/press-releases/adidas-terminates-partnership-with-ye-immediately).

Jewish people are not an economic system. No religious or ethnic population is mammon. Class criticism examines ownership, contracts, institutions, and power; scapegoating abandons that inquiry. A messenger to the poor who directs hatred toward a people has betrayed the message at that point. Restoration requires responsibility and changed conduct; applause cannot perform either task for him.

A fallible herald remains answerable to the people he would lead. That is what makes this figure human rather than divine, and what gives the comparison an ethical obligation rather than a fan club.

## Against Christendom, with Christianity in the argument

Our attack is on Christendom: the arrangement by which prestige and wealth acquire a religious blessing while the poor are invited to admire the arrangement. Christianity supplies part of the accusation. The good news to the poor embarrasses a church that treats poverty chiefly as evidence of insufficient ambition.

The artist who sings before that church may become its paid ornament, or he may make its contradiction audible. The critic has the same choice. I do not ask Kanye to confer salvation upon us. I ask whether his work can help people recognize their creative power and claim the conditions under which it can flourish.

There is our bloodless revolution: more people able to make, own, speak, associate, and refuse humiliation. Let the stronger man prove his strength by increasing that number. Let the writer prove his loyalty by continuing to ask who remains outside.

The poor need good news that changes what they can do tomorrow. The microphone is a beginning. The ownership of the microphone is the next question.
