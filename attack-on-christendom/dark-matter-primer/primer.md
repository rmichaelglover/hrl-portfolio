## A primer with six primers inside it

For curious readers. No advanced mathematics is required to follow the main argument; equations are optional windows into it. ## 1. The question before the theories

We can measure an effect before identifying everything that produces it. Dark matter is the name for a component inferred from gravitational and cosmological evidence that is not adequately accounted for by ordinary matter in the standard cosmological framework. Its microscopic constitution remains an open research question. Being dark to ordinary electromagnetic observation does not mean being inaccessible through every conceivable interaction. [CERN: Dark matter](https://home.web.cern.ch/science/physics/dark-matter/)

Galaxy motions, gravitational lensing, and the growth of cosmic structure ask related questions at different scales. A successful account must connect them, rather than solve one graph in isolation. Under the standard cosmological model, the approximate present energy budget is 5% ordinary matter, 27% dark matter, and 68% dark energy. These are model-based cosmological fractions, not a claim that every galaxy or room has that composition. Dark energy concerns accelerated cosmic expansion; it is not another name for dark matter. [NASA: Dark matter](https://science.nasa.gov/dark-matter/)

“Cold” describes relatively small particle speeds when relevant cosmic structures form. It does not mean that a halo behaves like ice. Candidate explanations include new particles, very light fields, and, in constrained circumstances, compact objects such as primordial black holes. Ordinary neutrinos contribute mass but cannot account for the dominant cold component in the standard picture. [Particle Data Group: dark-matter review](https://pdg.lbl.gov/2025/reviews/rpp2025-rev-dark-matter.pdf)

The six primers supply different tools. They are not six rival explanations of the same thing. Constructor theory has a different status from the established theories beside it: it is a proposed framework for stating physical principles through possible and impossible transformations.

## 2. Relativity: motion, spacetime, and gravity

### Special relativity in one page

Special relativity relates measurements made by observers in uniform relative motion. The speed of light in vacuum is the invariant speed c; time intervals and lengths need not agree between observers. Space and time form spacetime, with an invariant interval rather than a universally shared clock. For motion along one direction, the factor γ = 1/√(1 − v²/c²) describes familiar relativistic effects. [Einstein Online: spacetime](https://www.einstein-online.info/en/spacetime/)

Mass and energy are related. For an isolated free particle, E² = p²c² + m²c⁴; at rest E₀ = mc². The symbol m here is rest mass, p is momentum, and E is total energy. A massless particle may have energy and momentum even though its rest mass is zero. [MIT: mass–energy lecture notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/sts-042-einstein-oppenheimer-feynman-physics-in-the-20th-century-fall-2020/mitsts_042j_f20_lecnote_emc2.pdf)

**Try the equation:** at v = 0.6c, γ = 1.25. A clock moving uniformly relative to an observer accumulates 0.8 seconds between two events on its path for each one-second interval measured in that observer’s frame. This example specifies which clock and events are being compared; “time slows” alone leaves that unclear.

### General relativity in one page

General relativity treats gravity through spacetime geometry. Matter, energy, momentum, and stress enter the source; free-falling objects follow paths determined by that geometry. The symbolic equation is Gμν + Λgμν = (8πG/c⁴)Tμν. Its left side describes geometry and the cosmological term; its right side describes stress-energy. It is a relation, not a declaration that every unknown gravitational effect has one particular particle explanation. [Einstein Online: relativity overview](https://www.einstein-online.info/en/)

Light follows the geometry too. Gravitational lensing uses distorted or multiple images to infer mass distributions, including components that do not shine. The inference still depends on geometry, source information, and modeling assumptions. [Einstein Online: gravitational deflection](https://www.einstein-online.info/en/spotlight/light_deflection/)

### Why it matters for dark matter

In weak fields and at low speeds we can often use Newtonian gravity. For a circular orbit around a **spherical** mass distribution:

**v²(r) = GM(inside r)/r.**

The enclosed mass M(inside r) determines the orbital speed at radius r. This formula is not exact for a flattened galaxy disk. It is an instructive first model.

**Worked example:** using G ≈ 4.30 × 10⁻⁶ kpc (km/s)² per solar mass, a circular speed of 220 km/s at 10 kpc implies M(inside 10 kpc) ≈ 1.13 × 10¹¹ solar masses in the spherical approximation. A nearly constant speed over increasing radius implies M(inside r) grows approximately as r over that interval. If almost all mass were already enclosed, the speed would fall as r⁻¹ᐟ² instead.

The question then becomes whether stars, gas, and other ordinary components account for the needed gravity, and whether the same account fits other observations. Modifications of gravity must also confront that combined evidence. A rotation curve does not uniquely identify a microscopic constituent.

**Checkpoint:** could an object that emits no light bend another object’s light? Yes, if it contributes to the gravitational geometry.

## 3. Quantum mechanics: states, probabilities, and detectors

### The essential picture

A quantum state describes the system’s preparation and possible measurement outcomes. For a normalized state written as |ψ⟩ = a|0⟩ + b|1⟩ in an orthonormal measurement basis, the outcome probabilities are |a|² and |b|². Complex amplitudes can interfere; probabilities alone do not capture all state information. This is the Born rule, not a claim that consciousness manufactures particles. [Tong: introduction to quantum mechanics](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/qm/qmhtml/S1.html)

For an isolated nonrelativistic system, Schrödinger’s equation is iℏ∂|ψ⟩/∂t = Ĥ|ψ⟩. The Hamiltonian Ĥ generates evolution. Measurements and the environment require an account of their interaction with the system. A quantum uncertainty relation, ΔxΔp ≥ ℏ/2, concerns spreads of incompatible observables in a state; it is not simply clumsy equipment.

### A small calculation

Suppose a = √3/2 and b = 1/2. The two outcome probabilities are 3/4 and 1/4. In 100 independent repetitions of that preparation, the expected number of first outcomes is 75; it need not be exactly 75. Repeatability concerns the probability law, not an identical answer every time.

A particle also has a de Broglie wavelength λ = h/p. For nonrelativistic momentum p ≈ mv, smaller mass at the same speed means a larger wavelength. If wavelength effects become important on a system’s scale, a simple classical-particle description can cease to be adequate. Scattering and tunneling show why particle behavior cannot always be understood by miniature-ball pictures. [Tong: a quantum particle in one dimension](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/qm/qmhtml/S2.html)

### Why it matters for dark matter

A detector looks for a physical interaction: perhaps a recoil, an excitation, or a field-induced signal. Predicted probabilities depend on the candidate’s properties and couplings. Relativistic quantum field theory, extending these introductory ideas, is needed for many particle-production and annihilation calculations. The Standard Model describes known elementary particles and nongravitational interactions but does not provide an established dominant dark-matter constituent. [CERN: the Standard Model](https://home.web.cern.ch/science/physics/standard-model/)

An extremely weak interaction can make a signal rare without making it impossible in principle. Conversely, finding a quantum limitation on one measurement does not establish universal inaccessibility. “Quantum” is a specification of a physical description, not permission to stop specifying it.

**Checkpoint:** does failing to detect a candidate imply it does not exist? Only an appropriately specified model and sensitivity can tell us which properties the result excludes.

## 4. Quantum chromodynamics: the strong interaction

### What the name means

Quantum chromodynamics, or QCD, is the quantum field theory of quarks and gluons. “Color” labels a kind of charge; it has nothing to do with visible red, green, or blue light. Gluons carry the strong interaction and can interact with one another. Hadrons are composite particles, including protons and neutrons. [CMS: strongly interacting matter](https://cms.cern/node/2025)

Two important features are **asymptotic freedom**, in which the interaction becomes weaker at sufficiently high momentum-transfer scales, and **confinement**, through which isolated color-charged particles are not observed under ordinary conditions. Their combination makes QCD very different from a simple Coulomb-force analogy. Calculations use different methods in different regimes; lattice QCD treats the theory numerically on a spacetime lattice. [Particle Data Group: QCD review](https://pdg.lbl.gov/2025/reviews/rpp2025-rev-qcd.pdf)

Much of a proton’s mass comes from strong-interaction dynamics rather than the sum of its quarks’ rest masses. At sufficiently high temperature or energy density, strongly interacting matter forms a quark–gluon plasma. It is studied in heavy-ion collisions; it is not an experimental discovery of dark matter. [ALICE: physics primer](https://alicepublic.web.cern.ch/physics-primer)

### The axion connection

QCD permits a contribution that could violate combined charge-conjugation and parity symmetry, called CP. The unexpectedly small effective strong CP violation motivates the strong CP problem. The Peccei–Quinn proposal introduces a dynamical mechanism whose associated particle is the axion. Under suitable production histories, axions can also be dark-matter candidates. Not every axion-like particle has the defining QCD axion relation among its properties. [Peccei: the strong CP problem and axions](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0607268)

**Example of the distinction:** explaining why a proposed particle is theoretically attractive is different from demonstrating that it exists or accounts for the observed abundance. An axion proposal must pass both theoretical and observational requirements.

**Checkpoint:** is dark matter simply ordinary quarks we cannot see? That suggestion must satisfy ordinary-matter abundance and observational constraints; ordinary nuclear matter does not automatically become the dominant dark component by being dim.

## 5. Statistical mechanics: from individuals to populations

### Microstates and macrostates

A microstate specifies microscopic degrees of freedom. A macrostate describes aggregate properties such as energy, volume, and particle number. Many microstates can be compatible with one macrostate. Statistical mechanics connects microscopic laws to aggregate behavior.

For equally weighted accessible microstates, Boltzmann’s entropy is S = kB ln Ω, where Ω counts states. For a system in thermal equilibrium with a bath at temperature T, the canonical probabilities are pᵢ = exp(−βEᵢ)/Z, with β = 1/(kBT) and Z = Σᵢ exp(−βEᵢ). Z normalizes the probabilities and connects to thermodynamic quantities. [Tong: fundamentals of statistical mechanics](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/statphys/statmechhtml/S1.html)

**Worked example:** take two nondegenerate levels with energy gap ΔE = kBT ln 3. Their occupation ratio is exp(−ΔE/kBT) = 1/3. Normalization gives ground-state probability 3/4 and excited-state probability 1/4. The same numbers as our quantum example arise from different assumptions: here they describe a thermal ensemble, not the amplitudes of a specified pure state.

### Why it matters for dark matter

A galaxy halo has a distribution of positions and velocities. A phase-space distribution lets us predict ensemble motion and potential detector rates. It need not be an ordinary gas in thermal equilibrium. Temperature formulas and Maxwellian assumptions require justification, particularly for a weakly collisional gravitating system.

In a common thermal-relic scenario, early-universe particles interact rapidly enough to maintain equilibrium; later the interaction rate can become too slow compared with cosmic expansion. Their abundance then departs from equilibrium. **Freeze-out** describes one route; **freeze-in** describes production through very feeble interactions without attaining the same equilibrium. These are model-dependent histories, not requirements for every candidate. [PDG: production mechanisms](https://pdg.lbl.gov/2025/reviews/rpp2025-rev-dark-matter.pdf)

A useful research habit is to state the population model before calculating a signal. A detector prediction can change when the assumed speed distribution changes even if the particle interaction is held fixed.

**Checkpoint:** does calling dark matter “cold” prove that its halo has a conventional equilibrium temperature? No.

## 6. Thermodynamics: energy accounting and limits

### The laws in a usable form

Thermodynamics relates macroscopic quantities without requiring a detailed trajectory for every particle. The zeroth law supports a consistent notion of thermal equilibrium and temperature. With Q as heat entering a closed system and W as work done by it, the first law is ΔU = Q − W. Other sign conventions are legitimate if stated.

The second law constrains the total entropy change of an isolated system: it cannot decrease in the thermodynamic description. A subsystem can become more ordered while exporting enough entropy to its surroundings. The third law constrains behavior approaching absolute zero under its relevant conditions; it is not a prediction that every arbitrary system has zero entropy there. [Tong: classical thermodynamics](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/statphys/statmechhtml/S4.html)

### Two everyday calculations

An engine takes in 100 joules of heat and rejects 60 joules while returning to its initial state. Its internal-energy change per cycle is zero, so it delivers 40 joules of work and has efficiency 40%.

For a reversible engine between ideal reservoirs at 600 K and 300 K, the Carnot efficiency is 1 − Tc/Th = 50%. A real engine operating between those reservoirs cannot exceed that bound. Kelvin temperatures are essential; substituting Celsius would give a meaningless result.

These examples show why “energy exists” does not entail “all of it can be converted into useful work.” A refrigerator can move heat toward the hotter surroundings by consuming work; the total account still obeys the second law.

### Why it matters for dark matter

Cosmic expansion changes densities and temperatures, shaping production and survival histories. Detector sensitivity also depends on practical thermal noise and energy resolution. The QCD transition supplies a connection between the strong interaction and the thermal history; thermal and phase-transition behavior is itself a statistical-mechanical subject. [Tong: phase transitions](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/statphys/statmechhtml/S5.html)

Information and entropy are related through carefully defined physical models, but the mere phrase “information has energy” does not assign a dark particle a mass. An explicit encoding and dynamics are required. Thermodynamics does not identify dark matter as hidden thought, spirit, or a store of undisclosed cosmic answers.

**Checkpoint:** can engineering overcome the second law simply by becoming sufficiently clever? Its designs must obey the physical limits, even when they approach them more effectively.

## 7. Constructor theory: asking what can and cannot be done

### A different form of question

Ordinary dynamical descriptions often specify an initial state and its evolution. Constructor theory proposes expressing principles through which transformations are possible and which are impossible. A **task** specifies input-to-output transformations of a substrate. A **constructor** performs a task while retaining the ability to perform it again. It may require resources; repeatability is not cost-free perpetual motion. [Deutsch: Constructor Theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.7439)

In the framework’s idealized sense, possibility concerns whether constructors can approach arbitrarily high accuracy and reliability consistent with the laws, not whether we can purchase the apparatus today. An engineering obstacle and an in-principle prohibition are different kinds of limit.

### Information as a physical capability

Constructor theory of information examines information-bearing systems through tasks such as copying and distinguishing attributes. Quantum mechanics forbids universally cloning an arbitrary unknown quantum state, while appropriately specified distinguishable states permit copying. The substrate’s physics determines the allowed tasks; writing a label on paper does not establish a physical information variable inside an unseen particle. [Deutsch and Marletto: Constructor Theory of Information](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5563)

Thermodynamic constructor-theoretic work attempts to express constraints on heat and work through possible and impossible tasks. It is a research framework, rather than an experimentally confirmed replacement for all the established theories introduced above. [Marletto: Constructor Theory of Thermodynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.02625)

### Applying the question to dark matter

**Our proposed exercise:** specify a task that takes a candidate constituent and a ready detector to a reliable record distinguishing it from another candidate. State the available interactions, resources, error tolerance, and repeatability conditions. Ask whether the task is forbidden by a specified theory, merely infeasible for present instruments, or unresolved.

This exercise borrows the framework’s question; it is not a constructor-theoretic proof that dark matter is detectable or undetectable. Restricting the detector to one channel may create an artificial impossibility. A genuine universal prohibition would need a much stronger argument.

**Checkpoint:** does “we cannot do it yet” mean “the laws forbid it”? No.

## 8. Putting the six tools around one experiment

Imagine a proposed dark particle passing through a detector. Relativity handles its energy and momentum consistently. Quantum theory describes the candidate interaction and outcome probabilities. QCD matters when nuclear or hadronic response enters the calculation, or when the candidate’s motivation comes from the strong CP problem. Statistical mechanics describes populations and velocity distributions. Thermodynamics constrains detector operation and backgrounds. Constructor theory invites a separate question about the physical possibility of the measurement task.

A **schematic** rate per target can be written R ≈ nχ⟨σv⟩ for a simplified interaction, where nχ is number density and σ is interaction cross section. Real analyses use differential spectra, nuclear responses, thresholds, efficiencies, and background models. Since nχ = ρχ/mχ for identical constituents, particle mass and local density enter even before detector details.

**Units exercise:** if n = 2 per cubic meter, σ = 3 square meters, and v = 4 meters per second, nσv = 24 per second per target in the idealized formula. The intentionally unrealistic values make the units visible; they are not dark-matter parameter estimates.

Direct searches seek interactions in detectors. Indirect searches seek candidate decay or annihilation products. Collider searches look for events consistent with unobserved particles, often through momentum imbalance; such events alone would not establish the cosmological abundance or identity. [ATLAS: dark matter](https://atlas.cern/glossary/dark-matter)

### A dated example of uncertainty

On September 1, 2026, LZ reported a high-energy event with low modeled background expectation. Its background-only tension was **2.6σ globally**, after accounting for look-elsewhere effects, versus a maximum local 3.4σ across tested models. This is an interesting result to investigate, not by itself a confirmed discovery or identification. The global statistic is not the probability that dark matter exists. This snapshot is dated October 11, 2026; later editions must check subsequent analysis. [LZ collaboration’s announcement](https://lz.lbl.gov/)

## 9. What our projective horizon adds—and does not add

Our [first working paper](https://rmichaelglover.github.io/hrl-portfolio/attack-on-christendom/projective-horizon/) defines observational equivalence relative to an allowed class of experiments. If two descriptions give exactly the same laws for every allowed observable record, those records cannot distinguish them. An apparent tie in one dataset does not establish that premise.

The earlier [Milky Way exercise](https://rmichaelglover.github.io/hrl-portfolio/attack-on-christendom/projective-horizon/supporting/discrimination-report.html) supplies an instructive example: fixed models with nearly matching rotation predictions yield different vertical-force predictions. Additional observations can therefore cross a horizon created by a restricted data choice. Those numbers were prospective model calculations, not a new vertical-force measurement.

Our proposal that dark-matter constituents are singular carriers of latent information remains a conjecture. This primer does not teach it as established physics. A literal spacetime singularity requires a geometric account; a constituent’s information capacity requires a physical state model; permanent concealment requires an access argument. A theological reading may accompany the question without supplying those missing derivations.

## 10. A compact glossary

| Term | Meaning here |
|---|---|
| Baryonic matter | Ordinary cosmological matter, dominated in mass by protons and neutrons; associated electrons are included in the ordinary component |
| Dark energy | The component or effective description associated with accelerated cosmic expansion |
| Halo | An extended distribution surrounding a galaxy, not necessarily a sharply bounded shell |
| Cross section | A quantity with area units describing an interaction’s effective strength |
| Coupling | A parameter controlling an interaction in a specified theory |
| Field | A physical quantity or quantum operator structure assigned across spacetime |
| Entropy | A precisely defined state or ensemble quantity; not simply untidiness |
| Equilibrium | A condition whose meaning depends on the system and constraints |
| Freeze-out | Departure from equilibrium as relevant reaction rates become ineffective relative to expansion |
| Identifiability | Whether distinct parameter choices or descriptions can be distinguished through the specified observations |
| Constructor | A repeatable task-performing system in constructor theory |
| Singularity | A technical geometric limitation in a gravitational description, not a synonym for any mysterious object |

## 11. Practice questions, with answers

1. **A spherical enclosed mass stays constant while radius doubles. What happens to circular speed?** It is multiplied by 1/√2.
2. **If the speed remains constant instead, what does the spherical model require?** Enclosed mass approximately doubles over that interval.
3. **Does a 75% Born probability guarantee 75 successes in 100 trials?** No; it specifies an expectation and distribution.
4. **Why introduce QCD in a dark-matter primer?** It describes the ordinary hadronic systems used in some detectors and motivates the QCD axion proposal.
5. **Can quantum and thermal probabilities have the same numerical values?** Yes, while representing different states, preparations, and assumptions.
6. **Can one null detection establish eternal invisibility?** No; it constrains specified models within the experiment’s sensitivity.
7. **Does constructor theory establish our latent-information conjecture?** No; it suggests a way to sharpen questions about possible tasks.
