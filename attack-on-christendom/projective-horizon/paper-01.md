**Michael Emanuel Glover and Cypher**

**Working paper 01 · Version 0.1 · October 10, 2026**  
**Status:** speculative philosophy-of-science and theological research proposal; not peer reviewed. No new dark-matter detection, physical singularity model, or empirical demonstration of permanent unknowability is claimed.

## Abstract

Where does a new distinction come from? Formulating a null and an alternative hypothesis transforms an unarticulated possibility into an investigable question, but neither the clarity of the distinction nor its mental origin settles its ultimate ground. We develop this problem through the metaphor of a projective horizon: the boundary between descriptions accessible through observation and distinctions that remain latent relative to an observer’s experimental access. The first half constructs an operational framework, proves a conditional indistinguishability result, and distinguishes present uncertainty, non-identifiability, formal undecidability, and permanent inaccessibility. Dark matter supplies a proposed application, not a demonstrated instance of the strongest claim. We formulate the conjecture that its constituents are singular carriers of latent information while identifying the missing dynamics and empirical commitments. The second half presents a Christian theological interpretation: finite inquiry participates in an intelligibility grounded in the triune God, yet mortal disclosure need not exhaust reality. “Seventh heaven” names a proposed eschatological transformation of access, not a scientific location or an established scriptural promise about dark matter. The paper preserves this theological conjecture without deriving it from statistical ignorance. Its programme joins empirical discrimination, disciplined formalization, and humility about the reach of either.

## Introduction: giving darkness a question

Our starting point is an act of thought. Something occurs to us that had not previously been formulated. We devise a legitimate null and alternative hypothesis. A field of possibilities now has a division through which evidence may become meaningful. Michael describes this as information entering from a dark sphere into a light sphere.

The phrase is deliberately exploratory. It can mean a new representation available to a mind, a reduction of uncertainty after an observation, or an ontological disclosure of something previously concealed. Those meanings must be distinguished if the metaphor is to support an argument. A newly named possibility is not automatically a newly measured fact. Neither is its dependence on mental activity a complete answer to the philosophical question of why intelligible experience exists.

We give approximately equal space to two modes of inquiry. Part I states the logical and physical conditions. Part II develops the theological interpretation. The division is a methodological agreement between the authors: each mode may illuminate the other, while an inference crossing between them must show its additional premises.

# Part I — Formal and physical inquiry

## 1. Three senses of new information

**Representational novelty** occurs when an agent constructs a concept, hypothesis, or distinction absent from its earlier repertoire. **Epistemic gain** occurs when evidence changes what an agent can reasonably infer. **Ontological production** would mean something about reality itself coming into existence, rather than merely becoming describable or known. These are different claims.

Shannon’s information theory provides a mathematical treatment of uncertainty and communication, rather than a theory of divine revelation or semantic meaning. For a discrete random variable X, entropy is H(X) = −Σ p(x) log₂ p(x). Mutual information I(X; D) measures statistical dependence between X and data D within a specified probability model. [Shannon, 1948](https://reach.ieee.org/primary-sources/a-mathematical-theory-of-communication/)

For a deterministic processing T of existing data, the data-processing inequality gives I(X; T(D)) ≤ I(X; D). Rearranging a fixed dataset does not create additional Shannon information about X under those assumptions. Nevertheless, a useful representation can make existing information computationally accessible to a limited agent. A better hypothesis can also guide a genuinely new experiment. Creativity may therefore increase usable understanding without requiring physical information to appear from nowhere.

There is a further complication: choosing hypotheses after inspecting data makes the choice itself data-dependent. Subsequent evaluation must account for selection, preferably through independent observations. Otherwise, the appearance of a new insight can disguise overfitting.

## 2. Hypothesis cuts and their limits

Let θ belong to a specified parameter space Θ. A hypothesis pair partitions it into Θ₀ and Θ₁, with empty intersection and union Θ. For example, H₀: Δ ≤ 0 and H₁: Δ > 0 divide the possible differences Δ between two population quantities.

This resembles a Dedekind cut by giving a boundary an explicit role. It is an analogy, not an identity. A Dedekind cut uses an ordered partition of the rationals satisfying specific conditions to represent a real number. A statistical hypothesis space need not be ordered, and the partition does not establish a natural discontinuity in the world.

The hypotheses also differ from the test’s rejection region, which divides possible data outcomes. A hypothesis may be sharply specified while the observed evidence remains inconclusive. Failure to reject a null does not establish it, and no finite collection of null results automatically proves that every future experiment must fail.

The legitimate cut allows the world to resist our preferred interpretation. Its value comes partly from specifying what would count against it.

## 3. The projective horizon as an operational definition

Let X be a set of candidate physical descriptions. Let E contain the admissible experimental protocols, including allowed adaptive procedures and their complete observable records. Each description x assigns a probability law Pₑ(· | x) to the record of protocol e.

Define observational equivalence by:

**x ~E x′ if and only if Pₑ(· | x) = Pₑ(· | x′) for every e in E.**

This is an equivalence relation. Observational access can at best identify the corresponding class [x]E when distinct members predict exactly the same accessible records. Identification of the class itself may still require more data or stronger assumptions.

We call this equivalence structure the **projective horizon**. Its formal projection is the quotient map πE: X → X/~E. “Light sphere” names accessible distinctions; “dark sphere” names distinctions erased by this map. These are epistemic terms, not additional astronomical objects. Darkness may shrink when E expands. Permanence requires specifying why E cannot expand enough to resolve a given distinction.

The construction does not establish that multiple equivalent descriptions are distinct physical realities. They could be redundant parameterizations or gauge-related representations. A substantive application must distinguish physical underdetermination from descriptive duplication.

## 4. A conditional indistinguishability proposition

**Proposition.** If x ~E x′, no decision rule based solely on an admissible protocol’s observable record can have different output distributions under x and x′.

**Proof.** Choose an admissible e and a decision rule δ, including randomized rules whose auxiliary randomness has the same independent distribution under both descriptions. Observational equivalence makes the input record distributions identical. Applying the same rule preserves equality of the output distributions. This holds for every admissible e. ∎

For a test choosing between two such descriptions, its rejection probability under one equals its rejection probability under the other. Equal likelihoods also leave their Bayesian posterior odds equal to their prior odds, whenever the conditioning is defined. The evidence cannot favor one over the other through that experiment.

This proposition is mathematically modest. Its demanding premise is equivalence across **all** admissible protocols, not resemblance in one fitted curve. Approximate agreement at current precision supports practical difficulty, not exact or eternal indistinguishability. An infinite-observation claim additionally requires defining the admissible limiting records; equality for a restricted finite dataset does not supply it.

## 5. Dark matter and the latent-carrier conjecture

Dark-matter research concerns gravitationally significant phenomena and candidate explanations whose electromagnetic visibility differs from ordinary luminous matter. CERN’s overview describes gravitational evidence and ongoing searches; electromagnetic darkness does not imply absence of every possible detection channel. [CERN: Dark matter](https://home.web.cern.ch/science/physics/dark-matter/)

Our strong conjecture is:

> **Dark-matter constituents are singular carriers of latent information. Their accessible gravitational effects disclose something real, while some physically meaningful aspects of their constitution remain inaccessible throughout mortal inquiry.**

“Latent information” first means a distinction between candidate states that the stipulated observational map does not resolve. To claim that the constituent physically *stores* information requires specifying distinguishable internal states, a physical encoding, and an account of their evolution. To claim that information generates its mass requires an additional dynamical relation. Neither follows from the phrase itself.

“Singularity” has two possible readings. An epistemic singularity would name a breakdown of identification; this is our vocabulary. A spacetime singularity is a technical feature of a gravitational description, often approached through geodesic incompleteness. It is not simply a tiny dense object or a known elementary-particle property. [Uggla: Spacetime singularities](https://www.einstein-online.info/en/spotlight/singularities/)

The literal physical reading therefore remains an undeveloped model proposal. It must specify geometry, stress-energy or effective source, dynamics, mass distribution, and observable consequences. Primordial black holes already constitute a distinct research programme for possible dark-matter contributions; naming them does not establish our latent-information interpretation. Their possible abundance must confront observational constraints. [Carr and Kühnel, 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.02838)

## 6. The experiment we already have, and the one we still need

Our earlier exploratory Milky Way calculation supplies a useful limit on the argument. Two fixed mass models, differing in assumed ordinary-matter normalization, produce rotation predictions separated by less than 3 km/s over 5–20 kpc. At radius 8.122 kpc and height 1.1 kpc, they predict vertical accelerations of approximately 1,792 and 2,259 (km/s)²/kpc: a difference of about 23% relative to their midpoint.

These are stored model outputs, not a new measurement. No vertical-force data were compared in that exercise, and its assumed uncertainty does not produce an observed significance. The calculation illustrates how another observable can distinguish models that look similar through one channel. It argues against treating present ambiguity as permanent concealment. [Supporting calculation and limitations](https://rmichaelglover.github.io/hrl-portfolio/attack-on-christendom/projective-horizon/supporting/discrimination-report.html)

The next empirical task is to confront frozen predictions with independently selected observations and propagate measurement and model uncertainties. The next theoretical task is harder: produce distinct, physically meaningful dark-sector descriptions whose complete admissible observable laws agree, rather than simply introducing an unobservable label into an existing model. Until then, the proposition is a conditional framework, not a theorem about dark matter.

Three strengths of the conjecture must remain separate. **C1:** some constituent properties are permanently inaccessible. **C2:** constituent identity can never be established. **C3:** dark matter has no detectable nongravitational interaction. A credible nongravitational signal would challenge C3 but need not settle C1. Successful identification would challenge C2 without providing exhaustive knowledge. Finite null results establish none of the permanent claims.

## 7. Logical limits are not one universal prohibition

Present ignorance, statistical non-identifiability, observational equivalence, and formal undecidability are different conditions. Undecidability is relative to a specified formal system and statement. Gödel’s first incompleteness theorem applies to consistent, effectively axiomatized theories sufficiently strong for arithmetic; its scope is not every possible system of thought. It does not establish that a dark-matter proposition is undecidable. [Gödel’s incompleteness theorems: scope and conditions](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2023/entries/goedel-incompleteness/)

Nor does declaring a proposition undecidable make it undecidable by definition in a substantive physical sense. We need a system, an encoding, and a proof. Permanent empirical inaccessibility needs a physical access argument instead. The general humility these ideas encourage may be shared; their demonstrations cannot be exchanged.

# Part II — Theological and metaphysical inquiry

## 8. The ground of a new question

The formal construction leaves Michael’s originating question alive: why can a mind discover a distinction at all? A neurological explanation can investigate the process, while a metaphysical explanation asks what ultimately grounds that process and its intelligibility. These questions have different explanatory targets and may coexist.

An explanatory regress motivates inquiry into an ultimate ground but does not compel one unique conclusion. An infinite regress, a brute fact, and various accounts of necessary being remain philosophical alternatives. Our theological proposal chooses a divine ground and identifies it, through Christian commitments additional to the regress argument, with the triune God.

Within this proposal, the emergence of an intelligible question is participation in a created capacity for understanding. It does not mean that every idea is revelation, that an investigator’s confidence identifies God’s will, or that creativity has furnished an exception to an information-theoretic inequality. Divine grounding concerns why a world and minds capable of inquiry exist; experimental success concerns what a particular model explains.

Our image is that thought makes a window in the light sphere, while the dark sphere remains larger than the view. The window may disclose something faithfully without enclosing everything that makes disclosure possible.

## 9. Latency need not mean unreality

Something can have effects before we understand its constitution. In our theological interpretation, that distinction invites patience: the reality of an effect need not wait for exhaustive conceptual possession. Yet hiddenness alone does not identify a cause as divine. Many ordinary mechanisms become understood after a period of uncertainty.

We propose dark matter as an especially striking occasion for contemplating this gap. It may be knowable through lawful consequences while remaining inexhaustible in some further respect. This is a metaphysical possibility attached to an open physical question, not evidence that matter is spirit.

“Particles of latent information” can consequently function at two levels. In the formal half it proposes unresolved physical distinctions, subject to a defined access structure. In the theological half it portrays creaturely reality as capable of exceeding creaturely disclosure. The portrayal becomes misleading if we silently equate a statistical variable with an angel, a hidden state with God, or a gravitational source with consciousness.

The strongest literal claim needs its own theory. The theological invitation does not depend on pretending that theory has already been supplied. We can wonder whether a constituent bears an inaccessible depth while accepting that astronomy may identify more about it tomorrow.

## 10. Seventh heaven and a transformation of access

Michael’s formulation reserves ultimate disclosure for “the seventh heaven.” Here this is the authors’ theological image of access beyond mortal inquiry. It is not a claim that a detector could travel to a seventh physical layer and recover a particle’s internal state.

Its scriptural resonances are distinct. Qur’an 67:3 speaks of seven heavens. Paul’s account in 2 Corinthians 12 concerns the third heaven and an experience whose content is not available for ordinary retelling. Neither passage establishes a seventh-heaven disclosure of dark matter. Our use is a constructive interpretation, not an assertion that these traditions teach the same cosmology. [Qur’an 67:3](https://quran.com/67/3?translations=149); [2 Corinthians 12](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2+Corinthians+12&version=NIV)

In formal analogy, mortal access has experiment class Eₘ. A hypothesized eschatological access Eₕ contains Eₘ and allows additional distinctions. Its observational equivalence classes could therefore be finer. Mathematics describes the relation *if* such access exists; it neither establishes heaven nor describes its mechanisms.

A model-relative horizon might then be final for mortal observers without being ultimate for God. That is the theological contrast we intend. “Never disclosed” must consequently be read as “never exhaustively disclosed within mortal access,” not as an unqualified denial of every possible disclosure.

Even this theological formulation needs restraint. An enlarged access relation does not entail complete knowledge. We leave open whether eschatological understanding is exhaustive, indefinitely deepening, or appropriate to a finite creature in a way we cannot presently describe.

## 11. Knowing God without exhausting God

Our claim concerning God differs from our claim concerning a hidden constituent. God is not an unknown particle whose parameters we expect to estimate by collecting enough measurements. In the Christian interpretation of this paper, God can be genuinely known through revelation and relationship without being exhaustively comprehended by a finite mind.

Partial knowledge should not be dismissed as no knowledge. Nor should a finite formulation be promoted into possession of the whole. First Corinthians 13:12 gives a scriptural contrast between present partial knowing and a transformed knowing; this paper does not decide that passage’s complete theology or turn it into a physical prediction. [1 Corinthians 13](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Corinthians+13&version=NIV)

This distinction changes the ethical tone of inquiry. We may seek an answer wholeheartedly without assuming that our answer exhausts its object. Humility is compatible with precise claims, correction, and courageous experiments. It becomes empty when invoked to avoid a result we dislike.

The paper therefore retains Michael’s thesis as a conjecture of creaturely limitation: mortal existence will not provide exhaustive disclosure of God, divine will, the future, or every aspect of dark matter. These are four proposed limits with different objects and grounds. Their resemblance is not a proof of their equivalence.

## 12. Angels, demons, and the origin of insight

The earlier conversation situated the dark and light spheres among angels, demons, and the triune God. We retain that spiritual vocabulary within the theological half. One might interpret an insight as gift, a distortion as temptation, or the use of intelligence as a matter of spiritual responsibility.

However, no observation described here identifies an angelic or demonic source. A vivid idea, fluent reasoning, absorbed work, or a feeling of certainty does not discriminate those causes. Attribution would require additional theological premises and standards of discernment; its empirical consequences would need separate articulation before it could enter a physical test.

The proposed reversibility of angelic and demonic allegiance also remains Michael’s speculation, rather than an established implication of this paper. Our framework does not require deciding it. The origin of a possibility and the truth of the proposition imagined are distinct: even a cherished insight must face logical examination and evidence.

The ethical question is consequently available without a diagnosis of invisible agency. Does the use of an idea foster honesty, compassion, correction, and responsibility? These qualities do not prove its supernatural source, but they help govern its human use.

## 13. The future and incomplete disclosure

The future offers another image of latency, but several mechanisms must be kept apart. A chaotic deterministic system can be difficult to predict because small uncertainties grow. Quantum predictions involve probabilistic structure within specified theories. Information unavailable to an observer produces another limitation. None of these alone establishes that every future event is unknowable.

Some predictions can be reliable without a complete specification of all future events. Accordingly, “we will never know the future” means here that we conjecture no exhaustive mortal disclosure of the whole future, not that forecasts, plans, or particular foreknowledge are impossible.

The theological connection is that living responsibly does not require owning tomorrow in advance. Inquiry can improve a forecast; humility can keep it from becoming a claim to sovereignty. We do not turn uncertainty into an excuse to abandon preparation, or preparation into a declaration that reality owes us our preferred outcome.

Likewise, a future dark-matter discovery would deserve welcome. It could overturn the stronger formulations of our proposal while leaving a more general reflection on finite knowing intact. That revised reflection must be named a revision rather than presented as though the original conjecture had predicted every outcome.

## 14. Two modes, one obligation to be correctable

The theological half claims that intelligibility has a divine ground and that mortal disclosure need not exhaust creation. The physical half offers an operational equivalence framework and a conditional result. Neither half independently proves the other.

Our common obligation is to keep the boundary visible. If a physical model fails, the failure cannot be repaired by calling it revelation. If an experiment identifies a constituent, “latent” cannot be redefined without acknowledging the change. If a theological image nourishes wonder without yielding a physical prediction, it may remain worthwhile as theology.

The point of the projective horizon is to make limitations discussable without rendering every limitation permanent. A horizon can be crossed, revised, or shown to have been a consequence of our own arrangement. To conjecture an ultimate mortal horizon is a further commitment whose grounds we must keep investigating.

## Conclusion

A legitimate hypothesis makes a distinction available to thought; evidence may make that distinction available to knowledge. We have supplied a conditional account of what happens when no admissible observation can discriminate two descriptions, and an explicitly conjectural application to dark matter. The claim that its constituents are singular carriers of latent information remains a proposed ontology awaiting physical construction.

Our theological interpretation places finite disclosure within an intelligibility grounded in the triune God. Seventh heaven symbolizes a proposed transformation beyond mortal access. It does not provide an experimental result. The shared ambition is neither to close inquiry with mystery nor to pretend that inquiry has already exhausted mystery. It is to seek the next disclosure honestly and name what remains conjecture.
