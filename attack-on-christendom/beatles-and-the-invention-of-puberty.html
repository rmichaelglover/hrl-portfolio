Did the Beatles’ Nude Photographs Awaken an Age of Horny Teenagers?

Attack on Christendom · Satirical Opinion · October 9, 2026

Or, a Parliamentary Inquiry into Whether Adolescence Existed Before the Record Sleeve.

The Committee for the Prevention of Things Already Happening has assembled to determine whether popular music invented puberty. Its preliminary finding is that young people were perfectly tranquil until a record company introduced the concept of having a body.

A small correction must interrupt this magnificent theory. The celebrated nude cover belonged to John Lennon and Yoko Ono’s 1968 Two Virgins, not a group portrait of the Beatles. The distinction matters. If we are to indict an entire generation’s endocrine system, the clerk ought at least to enter the right defendants.

Nevertheless, the Committee proceeds. Before the photograph, it assures us, every adolescent spent the afternoon alphabetizing sermons. Shakespeare’s lovers were a printing error. Every blush in every century was caused by an overheated drawing room. The human race reproduced through a subscription service whose invoices have regrettably been lost.

Then came an album sleeve, and Nature, previously awaiting instructions from Apple Records, sprang into operation.

This is a splendid arrangement for the moral alarm industry. An ordinary feature of growing up becomes an imported contamination, conveniently attributable to whichever musician has most recently annoyed an uncle. The uncle can therefore remain a custodian of civilization without remembering anything whatsoever about being seventeen.

Our alternative thesis is less profitable: teenagers have always been horny, and teenagers will continue to be horny. History changes the costumes and the vocabulary; it has yet to abolish adolescence. A photograph may provoke a reaction. It does not supply humanity with a faculty that had been missing since the dawn of time.

Nor does an occasional so-called impure thought constitute a completed biography of the person thinking it. The mind is an untidy drawing room. Visitors arrive without appointments. One need not appoint each of them Archbishop.

The useful questions concern conduct: whether people respect another person’s boundaries, exercise judgment, and treat one another decently. A thought is not a deed; desire is not an instruction; somebody else is not obliged to participate. Those distinctions offer rather more moral assistance than making a young person feel permanently disgraced for noticing that they are alive.

So everyone chill the fuck out about occasional so-called impure thoughts. Kindly reserve the thunder for cruelty, coercion, and the gentleman who insists on delivering a forty-minute lecture while the tea goes cold.

The Committee objects that this will weaken its authority. We suggest it demonstrate its authority by finding the biscuits.

Puberty requires no anniversary edition. It has been on tour for quite some time.
