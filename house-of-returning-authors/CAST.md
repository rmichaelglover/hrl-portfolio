# The names and their echoes

These notes record the author’s evolving dramatic intentions. Associations are layered, not literal identities, diagnoses, genealogy, or accusations against relatives.

## Michael

A composite drawing on Robert Michael Glover (the author’s father), Michael Emanuel Glover (the author), and the scriptural archangel Michael as an image and exemplar. In the Karamazov analogy Michael carries Ivan’s questioning and intellectual rebellion. The comparison is a dramatic prompt, not a claim that every aspect of Ivan belongs to either real Glover.

## Emanuel

Manny’s better self, still imperfect. The author joins Alyosha’s compassionate religious orientation with Dmitri’s passionate, embodied, conflicted humanity. Emanuel may seek reconciliation without having already earned it. He is neither an infallible judge nor a claim that the author is literally a scriptural figure.

## Glover

The chorus and a symbolic father figure, with an echo of Fyodor Pavlovich in the Karamazov comparison. Glover also carries theatrical grandeur, craft, admired strength, and a critical image of empire. In the author’s allegory its Babylon aspect signifies the American empire’s destructive or corrupt aspect; the metaphor does not divide a country or its people into mathematically measurable moral halves.

The Shakespeare association has a historical foothold: William Shakespeare’s father, John Shakespeare, was a glove-maker. The grandeur of Shakespeare is the author’s theatrical association, not a claim of descent. [Shakespeare Birthplace Trust](https://www.shakespeare.org.uk/explore-shakespeare/shakespedia/william-shakespeare/william-shakespeares-family/).

The author also invokes the strength of an admired Marine cousin. That personal association informs the drama without making the cousin a spokesperson for imperial wrongdoing. No private correspondence or Facebook history has been imported.

## Mea culpa and apologia

The author wants the work to defend his existence and capacities while acknowledging his mistakes. An apology and a defense can coexist without either automatically canceling the other. “I exist” is a dramatic refrain, not a claim that the reader must accept every judgment the speaker makes.

The final Babylon dialogue remains the final dramatic exchange. Its figure is symbolic and does not identify any named woman in the earlier farces.

## Literary reference

The three brothers are Dmitri, Ivan, and Alexei (Alyosha); their father is Fyodor Pavlovich Karamazov. The novel’s brothers and father supply an analogy for the cast rather than a fixed psychological classification. [Dostoevsky, The Brothers Karamazov, Book I](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/28054/pg28054-images.html).

## Tara and the tea movement

The fictional Tara is the heroine: calm, politically perceptive, protective of women and girls, with an independent purpose beyond Emanuel’s development. Her Marian and dove imagery belongs to the author’s symbolic theatre, not to a claim about the real journalist’s identity or faith. Emanuel is both hero and antihero through his actions, ambitions, care, and capacity for correction. The author’s “bipolar bear” metaphor does not make a diagnosis a measure of goodness or evil. The dream’s divine titles are contested images, not confirmations of the author’s literal identity. Dasha’s cabinet role and intelligence-service past are invented and do not allege facts about any real chess acquaintance.
