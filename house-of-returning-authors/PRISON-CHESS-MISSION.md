# Glover’s Prison Chess Mission

*A proposed mission, not a launched program. No institution, tournament organizer, funder, or chess organization is represented as a partner. This scene is an aspiration, not a claim that income is available to people in custody.*

The board is set on a plain table. Glover stands beside it, marker in hand. Emanuel has left three empty chairs around the board, for people who have not yet been invited into the room.

**GLOVER:** Take the poorest of the poor. Put a chessboard in reach. Help people earn money legitimately through tournaments and chess influencing.

**MICHAEL:** Let the public see the skill. Let a good game open a door.

**EMANUEL:** Start by asking the players what they want. A board should be free to sit down at. No one has to tell a painful story, appear on camera, or win a tournament to belong here.

Glover draws three circles: **LEARN. COMPETE. CREATE.** He draws a fourth, beyond them: **EARN—WHEN IT IS ALLOWED, CLEAR, AND FAIR.**

**GLOVER:** Prize money. Lessons. Commentary. A channel people want to follow.

**EMANUEL:** Those are possible paths, not promises. The rules differ by institution. Some facilities restrict paid media work, business activity, publishing, internet access, and even the handling of prize money. We do not ask anybody to break a rule or work for free so that our project can look inspiring.

**MICHAEL:** Then we build two routes. One for what the institution expressly permits now. One for the day a player can take their skill outside: a club, a tournament, paid coaching, a chess column, a channel they control.

**GLOVER:** And if a player makes the lesson, the analysis, the story?

**EMANUEL:** Their name and work stay theirs. Ask before recording. Agree in writing before licensing. Show the payment terms plainly. Let the player say no without losing the board, the class, or anyone’s respect.

Michael writes beneath the circles: **NO GUARANTEED INCOME. NO SURPRISE RIGHTS. NO ONE’S DIGNITY AS THE PRICE OF ADMISSION.**

**MICHAEL:** We count the work that reaches people. We count the opportunities actually offered. We count the money actually paid, if payment is permitted. And we ask the players whether the project helped.

**EMANUEL:** We do not promise that chess cures a life, secures release, or guarantees a following. We can offer a serious game, real instruction, honest competition, and a bridge to people willing to make room.

Glover caps the marker. The three empty chairs remain at the table.

*This is a fictionalized mission statement in dramatic form. The practical concept is still in development; any real program would need co-design with incarcerated and formerly incarcerated chess players, facility approval, qualified partners, transparent rights and compensation, and a jurisdiction-specific review before it began.*
