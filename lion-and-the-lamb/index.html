Layers within the buffer

Buffer residents retain their natural teeth and claws. In the fiction, they receive extensive training to be generally friendly to people; friendliness is not a guarantee of safety.

  • Prey animals have separate refuges and independent routes.
  • Every animal can withdraw to a private retreat.
  • People have protected observation areas and restricted contact.
  • Tension triggers separation; trained staff oversee encounters.
  • Natural weapons remain intact in Zone 2.

Training, supervision, separation, and retreat are distinct layers. The sliders drive an optional synthetic model; no training-efficacy parameter is fitted or assumed to eliminate danger.

THE RULES OF THIS FICTION

Three zones. Different freedoms.

1 · The human world

Ordinary civilized life: homes, families, towns, farms, and familiar domesticated animals. This is the inner world, with ordinary human expectations.

2 · The living buffer

The guardians have abundance, play, and care. Wild animals retain their natural weapons. Extensive training encourages friendliness, while supervision, protected contact, prey refuges, retreat choices, and separation provide additional layers in the fiction.

3 · The wild-wild

Space and retreat matter. Minimal intervention and minimal permanent human settlement. National parks, national forests, and state parks inspire this outer landscape, though actual places have different rules and levels of management.

The landscape draws the boundaries.

A ridge can shelter a wild pocket. A creek can carry a corridor between gardens. A lake or pond can gather visitors. A canal, railway, highway, or dirt road can link human places while dividing wildlife habitat. Crossings, banks, edges, and quiet routes become smaller zones within the larger three. No feature is automatically a safe barrier or a usable wildlife corridor: that depends on the inhabitants and the place.

Where Manny’s original idea enters the model

The original inner-world daydream included filed teeth and claws. Manny then introduced a distinct buffer whose animals retain those natural weapons and receive extensive training, followed by a truly wild outer zone. The current visualization focuses on that three-zone geography. Anatomical alteration is not generalized to the buffer. The toy model does not prove training effectiveness or safe contact. No private home location is published.

Real-world distinctions, separate from the fiction

In real life, feeding or familiarity does not guarantee loyalty or safe contact, and captive predators are not reliable household guardians. The sources below explain why this story is not an animal-care proposal.

Evidence behind the first distinctions

  • Georgia DNR · Guide to Legal Pets: black bears are listed among species that may not be kept as pets.
  • National Park Service · Habituated bears: distinguishes habituation from access to human foods; emphasizes management of human behavior.
  • AVMA · The veterinarian’s role in animal welfare: opposes reducing healthy carnivore teeth except for medical treatment or approved research.
  • GFAS · Bear sanctuary standards: reference for professional bear care, not a private-yard blueprint.
  • GFAS · Position statements: intentional captive breeding is generally excluded from true sanctuaries, with limited exceptions.

Sources inform the distinctions; they do not supply or validate this model’s numerical coefficients. No affiliation with these organizations is implied.
