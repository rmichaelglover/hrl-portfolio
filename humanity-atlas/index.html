THE CONNECTION GARDEN

One point. A link. A little constellation.

Choose a shape to focus on its story. Lines connect shared themes; the filled triangle compares three gathering practices. This is a reading map, not a map of ancestry or geography.

🔬 Look closer: objects, labels & the relaxation engine

The eight cards are objects. Food, welcome, and land are overlapping labels. Focusing on a label makes it a new object of inquiry: the theme hub gathers its supporting cards below. Labels can overlap; people are never assigned to exclusive boxes.

Each independent theme channel starts from an editor’s relevance judgment. Sparse pairwise messages, a theme hub, and one explicit three-way factor refine those strengths. These are model affinities, not probabilities that a culture or story is true. Facts stay attached to their sources.

Three evidence states · Trool

Supported: both selected passages describe offering food. Unresolved / ISH: one passage directly influenced the other. Contradicted: “Genesis 18:1–8 never mentions food.” These judgments describe the scope of this reading exercise; they are separate from theme affinity.

FLAGS, SHAPES & BELONGING

A symbol has a story, too.

Flags name particular communities or places. A modern country flag is never a label for every person, an ancient society, or a whole continent.

Explore the UNESCO record ↗

A modern Indigenous flag

Luritja artist Harold Thomas designed the Australian Aboriginal flag, first flown in 1971. Its history belongs alongside the much older, diverse traditions of Aboriginal peoples.

A traditional emblem of connection

The Hiawatha Belt’s central tree and four connected squares convey the unity of five Haudenosaunee nations. This little emoji sequence is a learning cue, not a reproduction of the belt or an official flag.

Sources, care & room to grow
