# Chesshustlagaard
## A Peculiar Kind of Tournament Existentialist
### By Michael Emanuel Glover

*A comic essay, October 4, 2026. Developed in dialogue with Codex.*

“Life can only be understood backwards; this fork should have been understood two moves ago.”

That is Chesshustlagaard speaking. The sentence is ours, the anguish is familiar, and the knight is presently eating somebody's queen.

## Either / Oops

The evening begins in the Hourly Crazyhouse Arena. Two minutes, two seconds of increment, and a roomful of people whose relationship to my king appears insufficiently pastoral.

At 19:20, the screenshot says third of eight. Two arena points. Rating 1367. Hollis11 has won the displayed game, and the board resembles a neighborhood meeting at which every attendee brought a queen.

I am trying to learn. They are trying to deliver a parcel of checkmate directly through my front door. Both parties are pursuing their interests with admirable clarity.

“Perhaps,” says Chesshustlagaard, “the real opposition is between the person I intended to become and the move I just played.”

Then somebody drops a piece. In Crazyhouse, capture is less a funeral than a recruitment interview. The departed may return wearing the other team's uniform. Grief must make room for logistics.

## Fear and Premoving

The field includes ratings of 1907, 2129, and 2333 in the later screenshot. I am 1376. These are not decorative numbers. These are people who can look at a position and notice that my bishop has been writing its resignation letter for several turns.

I consider Swiss someday. The appeal is a more orderly encounter with my own inadequacies: rounds, opponents, an appointment book for dread.

But tonight is arena night. Finish a game, breathe, get paired, discover a new practical objection to the placement of my king.

Chesshustlagaard calls this the leap of faith. The opponent calls it a blunder. The board offers no comment because the board is busy being exact.

## The Resignation Unto Move Two

At 19:29, the next screenshot says first of nine. Four points. Thirty minutes and seven seconds remain. The displayed result is a win against sharwang.

The board shows an opening position after e4 and ...d6. I report that the opponent resigned on move two. The screenshot records the win and the displayed position; the move-two explanation comes from me. Let the modest distinction survive even inside the joke.

Two moves! An entire existential crisis, express delivery.

“I have assessed the position,” says our imaginary opponent, “and remembered another appointment.”

We do not know their actual reason. Possibly something ordinary interrupted the game. Nevertheless, the comic bureau has issued its finding: the abyss looked back, checked its calendar, and clicked resign.

Chesshustlagaard accepts the points with the solemn humility of a man who immediately checks whether he is now leading the tournament.

He is.

## Retire at One Thousand

Now comes the new rating: a batting-style number. Average is .333. Good is .666. Excellent is .999. The summit is 1.000, defined against the current version of Relaxfish under specified playing conditions.

These are proposed landmarks, not measurements extracted from tonight's screenshots. The ordinary Lichess rating remains its own number. Our new scale still needs a calibrated performance measure and a working implementation.

But the motto has already arrived fully dressed:

**RETIRE AT ONE THOUSAND.**

Reach the benchmark. Earn the laurels. Maintain your form.

The score may dip gently after setbacks. Improvement moves it upward. Reaching one should mean demonstrated parity with the chosen benchmark, judged through an agreed evaluation rather than one lucky game. The record should preserve which version was matched. An improved engine presents a new target without erasing the old achievement.

This is where Chesshustlagaard discovers the difference between a crown and a receipt. A crown announces a status. A receipt says what was earned, when, and against whom.

In my telling, my friend and fitness enthusiast Jeffrey Sim has co-opted the slogan. Its sporting application is excellent: earn a milestone and take pleasure in maintaining it. The imaginary marketing department adds a towel, a shaker bottle, and a knight doing lunges.

## The Sickness Unto Fork

The danger is that every mistake becomes a verdict on the whole person. Lose a queen, lose a game, lose the entire metaphysical argument concerning whether I should have taken up gardening.

Chesshustlagaard proposes a smaller unit of suffering: the position.

Where was the threat? What did I overlook? What will I recognize next time? The board is sixty-four squares; my embarrassment need not annex the rest of the universe.

A loss can become useful when the lesson survives into another game. A win can be pleasant without proving that every decision was brilliant. A two-move resignation can be accepted without founding a philosophical school upon its presumed inevitability.

Although we have, admittedly, founded a philosophical school upon the joke.

## Resting on the Laurels, Watching the Knight

Third becomes first. The rating gains nine points. The night remains unfinished. That is the whole available triumph, and it is enough to enjoy.

No final tournament victory is claimed here. The screenshots catch the evening in motion, with half an hour still on the arena clock. Our hero is temporarily leading, permanently learning, and vulnerable to a knight with excellent timing.

Chesshustlagaard straightens his imaginary waistcoat.

“I understand my games backwards,” he announces. “I would now like to play one forwards.”

The next pairing arrives.

Wings out. Watch the fork.
