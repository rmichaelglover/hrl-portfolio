    The laws of this habitat

    Freedom of movement

    Rooks, bishops, and queens travel across the whole board until blocked. Knights jump. Kings step. Pawns keep their team direction, may initially advance two squares, and promote at the far edge of the entire habitat. No wrapping and no Conway births.

    All friendly kings stay safe

    Teams alternate globally, White first. A move is legal only if every surviving king of the moving team is safe afterward. Castling uses the king’s original rook and home squares; en passant expires after the opposing team’s next ply. Kings are never captured.

    Local checkmate, global survival

    A checked king is mated only if no move by any allied piece can rescue that king. Its original army retires together, including pieces that crossed regions. Other armies continue. White wins only after all Black kings are checkmated; Black wins under the mirrored condition.

    Rational draws

    No legal move with no check is stalemate. If several kings are checked and each can escape individually, but no move saves them all, the habitat draws by royal deadlock. Other draws: bare kings, automatic threefold repetition, 100 quiet plies, or a 1,200-ply observation limit.

    This is an explicitly defined multi-king chess variant inspired by the cellular garden, not standard tournament chess. The 8 × 8, one-pair setting uses ordinary piece geometry and king safety. The habitat automatically applies threefold/50-move draws rather than requiring a claim. The forage policy is a seeded local heuristic, not a strong chess engine.
