# Future Reference — *Chess of Teenagers*

## Working title

*Chess of Teenagers: Whatever, Whatever, Man—That’s Just, Like, Your Opinion, Am I
Right? LOLZ*

This is a development note only. Do not begin drafting or connect it from the tween
novella until a future project explicitly chooses to do so.

## Proposed shelf

The first follow-up would be PG-13: teenagers inherit the Woodland board, discover
that confidence and certainty are different things, and use humor to survive difficult
games without treating one another cruelly. Its fantasy scale may be influenced by
epic journey stories and, occasionally, court-intrigue fantasy, while retaining original
realms, language, characters, and plot.

A much later finale could move through deliberately comic life-stage gates:

- 18: freedom meets consequence;
- 21: celebration meets responsibility;
- 23: the first grand existential crisis;
- 28: reintegration after chaos;
- 40: “Lordy, lordy,” adulthood notices itself;
- 50: “fifty, snifty,” competence with burritos;
- 61: the first serious health scare, handled without jokes at illness;
- 70: a reflective golden age;
- the end: mortality, legacy, and returning the pieces kindly to the box.

These numbers are chapter gates, not claims that every reader follows one life schedule.
Any adult volumes require separate audience labeling and must never be marketed as part
of the tween edition.

## Character and licensing boundary

An aspirational licensed edition could invite Anya Taylor-Joy and Netflix to participate
and could feature an authorized virtual portrayal connected to *The Queen’s Gambit*.
That version may exist **only** after written permissions covering name, likeness,
performance, character, series assets, publicity rights, synthetic-media use, approval,
credit, compensation, distribution, and sponsorship language.

Until then, use an original fictional adult chess prodigy with a distinct appearance,
history, voice, and name. Do not generate or publish Anya Taylor-Joy’s likeness, do not
use Beth Harmon as a character, and do not state or imply Netflix endorsement or
sponsorship. Inspiration may be discussed truthfully; affiliation may not be invented.

## Tone and dramatic grammar

The saga should feel historically grounded and mystically speculative, confusing and
straightforward at once. Chess supplies the dramatic grammar: clever forcing lines lead
to judgment, capture, exile, sacrifice, execution, and—in adult-only volumes—rare
non-graphic imprisonment or torture. Harm stays consequential, never eroticized,
instructional, celebratory, or casually inflicted for atmosphere. Court intrigue may be
sharp without pornography or promiscuity.

Recurring symbolic roles may include:

- the implicit and the explicit;
- the fish of fishes and fishers of fishers;
- the shepherd, the fallen star, and the outcast;
- the redeemed and the redemptive;
- the baptized and the baptist, used as literary or mythic motifs rather than a demand
  that readers adopt a belief.

The **Wise One** may be understood within this later saga as God, Goddess, Father God,
Mother God, a nonbinary Parent God, or the Holy Spirit. Different characters may name,
hear, question, or avoid naming the same mystery differently. Sometimes the presence is
implicit in mercy, conscience, wind, silence, or an unexpectedly redemptive move;
sometimes a later-audience chapter may speak explicitly. No single character's language
must invalidate another's dignity, and ambiguity should not be used to mock sincere
belief or sincere uncertainty.

The PG-13 volume should keep spirituality invitational and metaphorical. Theology,
politics, and mature violence belong—if anywhere—in clearly separated later-audience
books, with multiple viewpoints and no ethnonationalist program.

## Tiny Creek continuity

Tiny Creek is the small cause with enormous reach. A pawn move changes a file; a leaf
changes a current; a current changes a bank; the bank changes a road; the road changes
who arrives in time. The series can use deterministic chaos, feedback, bifurcation, and
sensitive dependence as story structures without pretending chaos theory predicts
individual lives.

The final image is quiet: after huge changes driven by tiny creeks, an older player
recognizes the first small choice that began the journey, forgives what can be forgiven,
repairs what can be repaired, and makes one careful move for whoever comes next.

## Vocational and fork-building track

The teenage edition may include practical, age-appropriate instructions for creating a
commercial or nonprofit-capable fork as a future vocational project. Each exercise must
identify separately:

- the software license and source-notice obligations;
- licenses for writing, artwork, music, fonts, datasets, and model outputs;
- trademarks, character rights, publicity/likeness rights, and endorsement language;
- contributor agreements and attribution records;
- privacy, accessibility, child-safety, and data-retention duties;
- the distinction between nonprofit status, open-source licensing, and permission to
  earn revenue—three related but non-interchangeable questions.

Learners can practice choosing a project name, retaining license files, recording asset
provenance, publishing source, writing a plain-language privacy statement, estimating
hosting costs, and selecting an honest revenue or donation model. Exercises must not
promise that a license alone settles every jurisdiction's employment, tax, fundraising,
consumer-protection, or intellectual-property law; professional review remains a gate
for a real launch.

The adult edition may take the same path further into cooperative and conventional
business structures, nonprofit governance, paid maintainership, grants, sponsorships,
procurement, contracts, insurance, moderation, security response, succession planning,
and sustainable shutdown or transfer. Its recurring question is not merely “Can this
fork make money?” but “Who carries its risks, who shares its benefits, and who can still
maintain it ten years later?”
