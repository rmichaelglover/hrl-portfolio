HRL for Responsible Cybersecurity

Chess-inspired adversarial reasoning for authorized defence and civilian resilience.

Read PDFPlain textEditable manuscriptLaTeX source
With great power comes a precise responsibility.

Protect people, preserve essential services, and make the limits of the system visible. This proposal applies hierarchical relaxation to defensive decision support in authorized environments.

A defensive advantage with explicit constraints

The framework connects provenance, service dependencies, competing role assignments, response-aware decisions and recovery assurance. Its mathematical objective minimizes residual security risk within an authorized feasible set constrained by service safety.

What the paper develops

  • A typed HRL architecture for observations, assets and service dependencies.
  • A robust decision objective with explicit authorization and harm constraints.
  • Calibration, abstention and a claim-specific uncertainty ledger.
  • A staged evaluation using synthetic environments, approved telemetry replay, isolated exercises and read-only shadow operation.
  • Governance, recovery, release scope and evidence needed for deployment.

The paper proposes future defensive research; it reports no cybersecurity benchmark or operational deployment. It contains no intrusion procedure or plan to disable foreign systems.

Sources include NIST cyber-resiliency engineering, the CISA StopRansomware Guide, and ICRC analysis of civilian harm.
