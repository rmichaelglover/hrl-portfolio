President
of the Board

The game was over. The room was not. Under the warm club lights, Manny claimed his seat with the same nerve he had just brought to the board.

The little chess room had gone loud in the good way. Chairs scraped. Somebody laughed near the coffee urn. At the far table, a kid reset the pieces before anyone could ask if the adults were done.

Manny turned his laptop so the people behind him could see the final position. His rapid game had been a mess for a while: an adventurous rook-pawn opening, a loose piece, and enough complications to make the chat scroll faster than the clock.

1. e4 h5 2. d4 h4 3. Nf3 h3

“You really just pushed that pawn three times?” Keema asked.

“I had a plan,” Manny said.

“You had a pawn,” Brandon replied. “Those are different things.”

The room laughed. Manny grinned and replayed the middle. His opponent had missed a chance to punish the opening. Then Manny found the queen move that changed everything.

10...Qd5!

Now the black pieces had a point. The position opened. Pieces came off. The rook found its way into the game, and the danger that had been aimed at Manny’s king turned around.

On-screen, the last move appeared.

24...Rxe2 · 0–1

“There it is,” said Miya. “You looked cooked, then you found the door.”

“I found a door,” Manny said. “Let’s not call it a whole house.”

He stood, rolled his shoulders, and looked around at the people who had stayed to watch. Their tables were different sizes; their ratings were all over the map. Nobody had to earn permission to belong there. The board made room for whoever pulled up a chair.

Manny“I’m Black. Black, Blackety Black. I’m Black. I’m the first Black president, motherfuckers!”

For half a beat, the room held still.

Then Keema lifted a captured white queen from the table. “President of this board, maybe. I’m calling the next game.”

“Motion recognized,” Brandon said, tapping a rook against the table like a tiny gavel. “The president has to defend the title.”

It wasn’t a speech about an office or a campaign. It was Manny planting both feet in the room and refusing to make himself smaller for anybody. The chessboard gave the boast a place to land: here, among these friends, in this game, under these lights.

He sat back down. Miya arranged the pieces. Keema took White. Brandon started the clock, then reset it because he had forgotten the increment.

“You ready, Mr. President?”

“I was ready before you found the clock.”

The first move crossed the board. Nobody needed a crown. The room already knew who had claimed the seat—and who was invited to take the next one.

The game began again, with enough room at the table for everybody.
