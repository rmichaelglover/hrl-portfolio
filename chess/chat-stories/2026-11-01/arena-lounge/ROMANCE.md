# Three Moves Across the Years
## A wholly fictional adult romance beside the arena chat

*Rowan and Jules are invented adults. They are not the people behind any username in the supplied chat, and this story does not claim anything about those players’ relationships or private lives.*

### First hello · Year One

Rowan was twenty-eight when the arena paired them with Jules, who was twenty-nine and playing from a kitchen table crowded with tea mugs.

They typed hello before either made a move.

Jules won a pawn. Rowan found a check. The position wandered into a draw neither of them had planned, then neither would resign. At the end, Jules wrote, “That was a ridiculous game.”

“A good ridiculous?” Rowan asked.

“The best kind. Rematch next week?”

The next week became Thursdays. Thursdays became a small constellation of messages: a puzzle, a song recommendation, a picture of the rain outside the window. They learned each other’s time zones and tea preferences before they learned how each other took coffee.

There was no grand declaration. There was the reliable green dot, then the gentle surprise of missing it.

### The café board · Year Three

They chose a chess café for their first meeting in person because a board gave nervous hands somewhere to go. Rowan arrived early and rearranged the pieces twice. Jules arrived with a copper-colored scarf and a smile that made the opening position feel suddenly irrelevant.

They played badly. They laughed well.

At move twelve, Rowan hung a knight. Jules touched it, then paused.

“Do you want the honest move,” Jules asked, “or the kind move?”

“The honest move. But say something nice about it first.”

Jules took the knight. “It was a beautiful blunder.”

Rowan laughed so hard that the neighboring table looked over. After the game they stayed for tea, then dinner, then a walk through the rainy streets with one umbrella and not quite enough room beneath it.

### Their small opening · Year Eight

By year eight they had a home with a porch, two mugs that did not match, a sleepy cat who considered the chessboard a warm square, and a guitar that Rowan played softly after Jules had fallen asleep.

They still played their Thursday game. Some weeks it was serious. Some weeks one of them deliberately tried an opening they had seen in a video and forgot what came next. They kept a notebook of favorite positions. On the first page Jules wrote: **The point is to come back to the board.**

They learned that a shared life was not a permanent winning position. It was a practice: ask, listen, repair, laugh, and leave room for the other person’s next move.

### A familiar endgame · Year Twenty-Two

By the time the garden had grown around the porch, the chess clock had stopped working. Neither of them replaced it.

Rowan set out the board. Jules brought tea. The cat took the chair. A black knight lay on its side where it had fallen last Thursday and neither had bothered to stand it up.

“Your move,” Rowan said.

“I thought it was your move.”

“Then we’re both waiting.”

“That sounds like a draw.”

“A draw by agreement?”

Jules laid a hand over Rowan’s. “A draw by tea.”

The old guitar rested by the door. The board waited in the afternoon light. No one needed to win the game to know they had chosen the same table, again and again, for twenty-two years.

*The arena chat supplied the mood and motifs of a lively chess room. Rowan and Jules, their courtship, dialogue, and years together are entirely fictional.*
