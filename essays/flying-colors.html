Flying Colors

Gaussian Mixture Models for the Optimization and Debugging of Large Excel Spreadsheets with Interdependent Columns and Semantically Significant Color Encoding

A story by Michael Emanuel Glover, developed with Codex

Fictional dissertation satire. The university setting, degree, performance figures, and research comparison belong to the story; this is not an actual GSU dissertation or institutional endorsement.
Yellow: pending
Yellow: approved
Yellow: Susan

She submitted her magnum opus to the business and accounting faculty at GSU: Gaussian Mixture Models for the Optimization and Debugging of Large Excel Spreadsheets with Interdependent Columns and Semantically Significant Color Encoding.

The title alone occupied three merged cells.

Her breakthrough was recognizing that the colors meant something, although nobody could consistently explain what. Yellow meant “pending,” except in Accounts Receivable, where it meant “approved,” and on one indispensable tab where it meant “Susan.” Most existing code ignored formatting altogether. Her system treated it as evidence, discovering latent clusters of financial meaning and, occasionally, Susan.

After years of research, she successfully defended her dissertation, graduated with flying colors—which her model classified with 94 percent confidence—and earned the right to call herself Doctor. She rode home on her bike, triumphant, while her tax return remained technically a distribution.

Unfortunately, reinforcement learning had solved the same benchmark at a higher success rate. Its policy was difficult to interpret, but the decisive action appeared to be:

“Ask Susan what yellow means.”

End scene.
