    A SMALL WORLD BEFORE THE WHOLE GLOBE

    The rules we’re trying.

    Sixteen pieces. Forty-five seconds.

    Each country has sixteen provinces and a full chess army. Timed deployment fills unplaced pieces randomly when the clock expires. This prototype uses fixed fictional countries; real country claiming and supplemental city spaces are not implemented yet.

    Spaces count. Kilometres tell stories.

    Rooks, bishops, queens, kings, and knights follow the movement atlas. Pawns have a forward route and two diagonal capture routes. Marked ocean junctions also permit a quiet advance along either diagonal, giving three forward choices while retaining two capture directions. No castling, initial double-step, or en passant in this first variant.

    Cross the ocean. Earn promotion.

    A pawn records departure from its own coast and promotes on an ocean space whose forward or capture route reaches a foreign coast. Allied coasts qualify. Choose the promotion piece before moving. Returning toward home does not qualify.

    Friends come with friends.

    Three requests per rival player, in each direction. Accepting merges both entire alliances immediately. Requests use no move. All surviving countries in one alliance share victory: one point each. This is a local point score, not an Elo rating.

    Armies disappear. Lanes open.

    After each complete global round, checkmate and stalemate are evaluated for all living countries together. Eliminated armies vanish simultaneously; new eliminations are assessed in further simultaneous rounds. Checkmate earns zero, stalemate half a point. Intervening moves can change the position before review.

    Polar crossings, visible exceptions.

    Longitude wraps around. Crossing a pole enters the opposite longitude’s polar row and reverses north/south travel. Routes preview the transition. This is an explicit game convention, not a claim that province borders create ordinary chess geometry.

    You must finish your move with your own king safe. If you have no legal move, pass and await the round review. Allied pieces block routes but cannot be captured; enemy kings are checked, never captured. A black-and-white checkerboard is a visual guide; bishops follow defined routes even where polar topology changes a cell’s color. The map and moves are fictional game mechanics, with no authority over real people or systems.
