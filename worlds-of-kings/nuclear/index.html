    ROYAL-CAPTURE CRAZYHOUSE · VERSION 1

    A queen has two choices.

    Capture, pocket, drop.

    Capture an opposing piece and it joins your reserve in your color. On a later turn, drop a reserve piece onto an empty, unscorched square instead of moving. Pawns cannot drop on the first or last rank. A captured promoted piece returns as a pawn.

    Kidnap the crown.

    Capture the opposing king to win. Kings are capturable, not royal: there is no check, checkmate, or king-safety restriction. Kings move one square and may stand next to each other. A captured king ends the game and never enters a reserve.

    Only queens can strike.

    Select a queen, choose Queen strike, then choose an enemy-occupied square along an unobstructed queen route. Preview before committing. The queen stays put. The target is captured and its square ignites. Ordinary queen captures remain available and start no fire.

    Three waves. Both armies.

    After each of the next three turns, fire spreads one further square by edge adjacency: Manhattan distances one, two, then three from the strike. Existing fronts also advance after a new strike. Any piece caught is captured into the other side's reserve, even the launcher's own pieces. Each square burns briefly, then remains scorched for the match.

    Territory is part of the price.

    Scorched squares block moves, drops, and sliding routes; knights can jump over them. Fire can cross scorched ground to reach later rings. At turn end, all existing fronts resolve before the result: if both kings are captured in that turn, it is a draw. If the next player has no move or drop, it is a draw.

    Détente keeps the game going.

    An offer uses no turn. The other player may accept or decline; making a move expires the offer. Acceptance permanently disarms all queens and stops active fire. Scorched squares remain. The game continues through ordinary moves, captures, and drops. The computer always accepts an offer.

    Pieces otherwise use familiar chess movement. Pawns can advance two squares from their home rank only if they have not moved; dropped pawns cannot double-step. Promotion offers queen, rook, bishop, or knight. This custom variant has no castling or en passant. “Loaded reserves” adds pieces to both armies; “The crossing” is a small invented position for trying the two queen actions.
