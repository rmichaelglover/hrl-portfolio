        THE RULES, NOT A LECTURE

        Play the world. See what happens.

        Take the king.

        Ordinary captures work, including kings and queens. Captured non-kings join your reserve in your color. Capturing a king retires that country; its remaining pieces stay on the map, inactive but capturable. The last living country wins. If every remaining king is captured in the same turn, the result is a draw.

        Only queens strike.

        A queen may strike an enemy on one of her unobstructed map routes. She stays put. The target is captured immediately. The next three actions spread fire one border-neighbor step farther each time. Strikes can win quickly; there is no artificial delay or guaranteed retaliation. Ordinary queen capture moves her and starts no fire.

        Reserves travel fast.

        Instead of moving, drop a reserve onto any empty, unscorched province or ocean space. All countries can use neutral land. A caught promoted piece returns as a pawn. Pawns promote at the poles or after the original ocean-crossing conditions. No check, checkmate, castling, or en passant.

        Fire has a trail.

        Scorched territory blocks movement, drops, and sliding routes; knights can jump it. Fire follows the mapped boundary-neighbor graph, including ocean spaces, and can cross earlier scorched ground. It captures any piece it reaches. Enemy pieces go to the launcher's reserve; the launcher's own caught pieces go to the first living rival in player order. Older fronts own overlaps.

        Agree, or don't.

        Every living country must accept détente. It uses no turn, disarms all queens, and stops active fire. Ruins remain. Captures and reserve drops continue. An unanswered offer expires on the next action. No peace bonus or forced lesson changes the result.

        A globe, with declared conventions.

        The game uses Fusion's Natural Earth v5.1.2 map and first candidate compass routes. These irregular routes are experimental. Real borders are geography; the nuclear rules are invented game mechanics. This is not a simulation of physical weapons or a proof of an optimal strategy.
