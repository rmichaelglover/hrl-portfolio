        Wildlife, flag colors & the imagined habitat model

        Native animal rosters are country-level inspiration, not province sightings. Emoji species approximations are named when you select a province or piece. When a native roster runs out—or has not yet been researched—flag-colored decorative symbols take over. Emoji artwork and its colors vary by device.

        The optional habitat layer reuses country population averages from The Lion and the Lamb atlas: tan = human-pressure candidate, green = buffer candidate, pale green = low-population candidate, grey = unknown. These are imagined zones, not measured animal habitat or safety.

        A MAP IS A MODEL. ITS SOURCES SHOULD BE VISIBLE.

        Geography first. Rules we can inspect.

        Borders remain borders.

        Province borders are simplified further for responsiveness; they are not replaced by squares. Ocean areas follow a twelve-degree latitude–longitude grid, clipped against mapped land. Coast-separated parts remain separate spaces.

        A compass, not a hidden guess.

        Candidate cardinal routes follow border connections. Diagonal candidates use corner contacts or two border steps. Click a region to inspect the choices. Emoji Wilds uses the first candidate in each direction for its experimental match engine. Coastal gaps and polar conventions still need playtesting.

        The original stays yours.

        Both earlier game engines are preserved. Emoji Wilds combines the globe and animal atlas, with a separate saved game. The earlier versions stay available.

        Made with Natural Earth, pinned to release v5.1.2. Its map includes countries, territories, and disputed areas and generally uses de facto boundaries. The displayed count is map coverage, not a count of universally recognized sovereign states. Population figures are source estimates, not live census data.
