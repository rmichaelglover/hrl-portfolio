Sixteen years of computer vision in domains where being wrong is expensive: railcar brake inspection, image-guided surgery, weld-defect detection, and real-time optical tracking for military training.

Every demonstration linked below is self-contained and runnable in the browser — no build step, no dependencies, no server. Most run on real published data, and several publish their own null results. My specialty is identification by constraints (relaxation labeling, after Rosenfeld, Hummel and Zucker): classifying objects by requiring the whole assignment to be mutually consistent, rather than by training volume. It is label-efficient, explainable, and built for the case where the interesting class is rare.

The applied case — automated inspection

Train-brake identification, Beena Vision Systems (2014)

Wayside machine vision for railcar safety inspection. Two grayscale images of a bogie corner — top- and bottom-mounted cameras — are stitched into a single view and reduced to line segments. Rather than classify each segment on its own appearance, the engine assigns every segment to a brake-component label by requiring the whole assignment to be geometrically consistent: perpendicular distances, slopes and intercepts, and ρ/θ relationships that must hold if the assembly is intact. Segments that fit nothing are absorbed by a noise label. The output is an inspection verdict, including flags such as “Top Brakepad NOT Visible.”

two cameras  →  stitch  →  line segments  →  relaxation labeling (geometry as compatibility, + noise label)  →  safety flag
