Make it yours in Audacity

The kit contains five lossless FLAC tracks, an import list, move labels, the original PGN study, and the generation script. FLAC preserves the WAV samples while making the download smaller.

  1. Download the Audacity kit and extract the ZIP. Keep the files together.
  2. In Audacity choose File → Open, then open Open-in-Audacity.lof from the extracted folder. If needed, select List of files in basic text format in the file dialog. The five tracks start together at time zero.
  3. Press Space to listen. Solo the drums, then bring in bass, chords, and the White and Black melodies. Adjust each track’s gain to find your mix.
  4. For move markers, choose File → Import → Labels and import Audacity-labels.txt.
  5. Save your Audacity project to keep your edits, then export audio to share your version.

The MP3 is a reference mix. Keep it muted if you import it alongside the five tracks, so you don’t double the music. The original mix peaks at −3 dBFS; watch for clipping when you raise track levels.

The five tracks

Download tracks separately, or get everything in the ZIP above.

  • Dusty drums — kick, snare, swung hats, and small fills.
  • Warm bass — low notes with a gentle pulse.
  • Soft chords — Dm9, B♭maj7, Fmaj9, and Cadd9.
  • White’s melody — higher notes, slightly left.
  • Black’s melody — lower notes, slightly right.

How the game becomes music

Each mainline half-move gets one beat. Destination files choose notes from D–E–F–G–A–B–C–D; moves landing on ranks 5–8 raise the note an octave. Captures play louder, and checks add a fifth. Black’s notes sit slightly behind the beat for swing. Engine variations are left out of the melody.

The backing and arrangement are artistic choices. This is one way to hear a game; chess does not determine a single composition.
