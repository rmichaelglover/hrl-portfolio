PROJECT CHARTER · 7 OCTOBER 2026

The lower chair first.

Having power does not, by itself, grant permission to exercise it.

Mercy toward perpetrators must not become abandonment of their victims.

Protect the threatened, stop the harm, preserve accountability, and end force when its necessity ends.

Before a consequential action

  1. Establish authority. Record who is entitled to authorize the action and the explicit permission they granted. Access, ownership of a tool, a moral label, or a feeling of divine authorization is not a substitute. Permission from one person cannot override another person’s rights.
  2. Bound the scope. Name the systems, actions, operator, protective purpose, and start and expiry times. Changes require renewed authorization. A grant for testing is not a grant for disruption, retaliation, or action on someone else’s infrastructure.
  3. Establish necessity and restraint. Identify the harm being prevented and consider less harmful effective alternatives. Protect people, private information, and essential services. A high confidence score cannot override these constraints.
  4. Keep responsibility human. Consequential execution requires the responsible operator’s approval within that scope. Record reasons, evidence, and uncertainty. Automated systems may not appoint themselves to a higher chair.
  5. Make stopping real. Recheck permission and necessity before execution and during continuing action. Expiry, revocation, an ended threat, or uncertain safety stops new or continuing intervention. A stop must not trigger an unsafe abrupt shutdown; follow the approved safe-stop and recovery procedure.
  6. Account and repair. Preserve appropriate evidence and an access-controlled audit trail, report consequences, and restore service or reverse restrictions when safe. Accountability does not authorize revenge.

How this applies to cyber tools

A future feature that changes access, isolates an endpoint, blocks traffic, or affects another person or system must enforce authorization on the service that performs the action. A browser checkbox, warning, or policy page is not an enforcement mechanism.

The service must deny execution by default when permission is missing, expired, revoked, or outside scope. It must support a safe stop, bounded execution, an audit record, and a recovery procedure. Test these controls before connecting the feature to real systems. Keep simulation and advice visibly distinct from execution.

The current White Hat Hackers and HRL cybersecurity pages publish research and reading links. They provide no external-system scanning, intrusion, or disruption controls. This charter sets requirements for future capabilities; it does not claim that such an execution system has been built.

Where the principle comes from

Manny Glover and Cypher developed this charter in a conversation about humility, protection, enemy-love, and the duty not to assume authority. “The lower chair first” is its spiritual image. The operational rule is explicit, lawful, accountable, and limited authority.

Chess captures and fictional conflicts remain game mechanics and stories. They confer no permission to act against real people or systems.
