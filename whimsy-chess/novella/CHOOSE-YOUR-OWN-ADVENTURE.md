# The Master Board
## A Choose Your Own Adventure Mode

This optional route sits outside the main twenty-five-game novella. Enter it only
when you want the chess moves themselves to choose the next realm. Nothing here
changes the recorded games: the reader plays or explores a legal move, checks the
matching signpost, and lets that choice color the narration.

The Master Board borrows the pleasure of a vast realm-map, a winter wood, talking
animals, hidden doors, towers, rivers, and long journeys. Its places and characters
remain the Woodland cast's own. This tween route contains no religion, politics, or
ethnonationalist argument.

### How to travel

At a marked position, choose a legal move before reading onward. Sort it by what the
move actually does:

- **Climb** — a check, capture, promotion threat, or forcing move. Follow the mountain trail.
- **Cross** — a developing move, pawn break, file-opening move, or exchange. Follow the bridge.
- **Shelter** — castling, retreat, defense, or a move that creates breathing room. Follow the lantern trail.

If a move fits two trails, the traveler chooses. If it fits none, Ella calls it a
**Wander** and sends the party through the nearest unvisited realm. No trail awards
better-person points. After each game, return to the Master Board and choose again.

### Ten doors on the Master Board

1. **The Kádas Cascade** — Raven Cliff Falls; daring pawns climb beside white water.
2. **The Queen Dab** — Mirror Meadow; a queen's flourish must still respect every attacked square.
3. **The Crab as White** — Tidepool Strand; edge pawns test how patiently a position can breathe.
4. **The Goldsmith Defense** — Lantern Marsh; Black learns when a strange first step needs ordinary development.
5. **The Stalemate Gambit** — Sleeping-Stone Vale; the smallest legal square can change the ending.
6. **Enter the Dragon** — Reed-Shadow Crossing; speed, control, and sportsmanship share one narrow path.
7. **Jerry Ball** — Brasswing Field; black-and-yellow banners frame a lesson in winning and losing kindly.
8. **Creepy Harriet** — Rootglass Forest; a familiar pawn gesture meets a classical structure.
9. **The Crawling Crown** — Crownroot Keep; a quiet shell becomes an attack only when the position permits it.
10. **CoachZ Crossing** — Longbridge Realm; an archive game asks when patient building must yield to urgency.

These are the first ten narrated doors, not a limit. Later archive games may gain a
door when they offer a distinct decision rather than a renamed copy of an old path.

For shorter opening-based routes, continue into **Woodland Chronicles: Attack of the
Narnian Clones**. Its Smaller Realms organize exact wins, losses, and draws by Kádas,
Creepy Crawly, Goldsmith/Carr, St. George, and Bongcloud families before opening eight
standard reference doors.

### Optional Chapter — CoachZ Crossing

The travelers entered Longbridge with a rating mountain rising far above them: 833
faced 1627. Ella covered the numbers with one wing. “They describe past results,” she
said. “They do not get to move a single piece today.”

White built the Creepy Crawly shell with `h3`, `g3`, `f3`, `e3`, `d3`, `c3`, `b3`,
and `a3`. Across the valley, Black developed quickly, castled long, and struck with
`...e4`. The shell had bought time, but time was useful only if it became development
and central control.

After `12.c4?`, the bridge began to shake. Black exchanged on f3, opened the d-file,
and sent a passed pawn toward b2. At move eighteen, however, `18...Ba2??` opened a
real escape route: `19.Qf5+!` could have crossed with tempo, followed by taking the
rook on d3. The higher-rated traveler had made a mistake. Mountains do that too;
stones slip on both sides.

Choose before continuing:

- **Climb with `19.Qf5+!`** — follow the forcing check, recover the rook, and enter a difficult but playable clearing.
- **Shelter with `19.O-O??`** — finish king safety, but allow `...Bxb1` and `...Rxd2`; continue into the lesson below.
- **Wander with another legal move** — compare it with the two signposts, then narrate whether it met Black's immediate threats.

In the recorded realm, White chose shelter. Black's b-pawn and rooks crossed faster
than the defenders could regroup. After `22.Qxc6?? Qxe3+ 23.Kh1 Nd5`, White left the
game. Ella did not call the expedition foolish. The opening experiment had revealed
its exact next lesson: when the center opens, stop finishing the picture in your head
and inspect checks, captures, and threats on the board.

The party archived the map, thanked the opponent through good sportsmanship, and
returned to the Master Board. A loss was weather. The discovered crossing was theirs
to keep.
