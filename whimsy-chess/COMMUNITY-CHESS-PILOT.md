# Tiny Creek Community Chess Pilot

## A real, small, mission-led starting point

The purpose is straightforward: help more children and families play, study, and enjoy
chess without making income, equipment, or an online subscription the gatekeeper.

The pilot combines three existing strengths:

1. inexpensive physical chess sets;
2. a small, welcoming local chess club; and
3. free browser-based Woodland lessons built from games that were actually played.

This is a proposed pilot, not an announcement that a nonprofit corporation, charity,
school partnership, or sponsored program already exists. No tax-deductible status,
institutional affiliation, or charitable receipt should be represented until the
appropriate written structure exists.

## Operating model

The working model is **free digital commons + inexpensive physical access + paid or
sponsored institutional services**.

- Core family reading, local hot-seat chess, and basic instructional material remain
  free to access online.
- A basic physical set has a target participant price of **$5**, when direct cost or a
  documented subsidy makes that price sustainable.
- Community partners may pay a transparent program fee for facilitation, preparation,
  transport, replacement equipment, or educator support.
- Grants, sponsorships, and voluntary contributions may subsidize sets and seats only
  after a suitable organization or fiscal sponsor can receive and account for them.
- Optional premium products may exist later, but basic participation is not designed
  as a sales funnel.

Five dollars is a target access price, not a claim that every set can already be made
and delivered for five dollars. If a set costs $8 to produce and deliver, the ledger
must show the $3 subsidy rather than hiding it as unpaid labor or an unexplained loss.

## First pilot: one table becomes several

Run one bounded pilot before forming a large organization or promising a platform.

### Scope

- **Partner:** one library, school, recreation center, neighborhood association, or
  established community organization;
- **Participants:** approximately 12–24 young people, with the partner determining
  appropriate ages and guardian procedures;
- **Length:** six to eight meetings;
- **Equipment:** 10–25 durable, complete chess sets;
- **Instruction:** selected Woodland trilogy chapters, replayed in Chess Maestro and
  tested over the board;
- **Staffing:** at least two responsible adults whenever the partner's safeguarding
  policy or circumstances require it;
- **Technology:** shared devices are optional; no participant account is required for
  the local reading and hot-seat tools.

### A simple meeting

1. **Welcome and setup — 10 minutes.** Count every set and pair players without
   ranking human worth by rating or experience.
2. **One exact game story — 15 minutes.** Read a short Woodland passage and identify
   its real position, move, and result.
3. **One practical idea — 15 minutes.** Practice a bounded topic such as checks,
   undefended pieces, development, promotion, or stalemate.
4. **Play — 30 minutes.** Friendly games with optional clocks and clear expectations
   for takebacks established before play.
5. **Reset — 10 minutes.** Return all pieces, record damaged or missing equipment,
   and let each participant name one move or idea they want to remember.

The trilogy supplies the lesson; Maestro supplies the replay; the board supplies the
test. Technology supports the human meeting rather than becoming the meeting's price
of admission.

## Minimum viable set

A pilot set should be inexpensive without being disposable. The initial specification
is deliberately ordinary:

- recognizable Staunton-style pieces or another clearly legible design;
- contrasting squares and pieces;
- kings that cannot be confused with queens;
- a board that lies reasonably flat;
- a complete inventory of 32 pieces, with low-cost spare pawns available;
- a washable or wipeable storage bag or box;
- no small magnets or other avoidable hazards in sets intended for younger children;
- one plain-language setup card and one link or QR code to the free lessons.

Before offering a set at any price, record:

| Cost | Per-set entry |
|---|---:|
| pieces and board | actual cost |
| storage | actual cost |
| instruction card | actual cost |
| assembly labor | paid, donated, or owner labor—identify which |
| transport and payment fees | actual allocation |
| breakage/replacement reserve | documented estimate |
| **true delivered cost** | **sum of the above** |
| participant price | target: **$5** when feasible |
| subsidy required | true cost minus participant price |

Do not use unsafe materials, misleading durability claims, or uncompensated labor that
someone was pressured to provide merely to preserve the five-dollar slogan.

## What to measure

Collect the minimum information needed to decide whether the pilot helped:

- sets distributed, loaned, returned, damaged, and incomplete;
- attendance per meeting and aggregate repeat attendance;
- number of complete games played;
- whether participants can correctly set up the board before and after the pilot;
- one or two age-appropriate chess checks, such as recognizing check, legal responses,
  and stalemate;
- participant and partner feedback about clarity, welcome, pacing, and fun;
- actual program cost, earned revenue, subsidy, and unpaid labor;
- changes the partner recommends before any repeat.

Avoid unnecessary names, photographs, precise addresses, behavioral profiling, or
biometric data. Publish aggregate results unless a partner has obtained appropriate,
specific permission for something more.

## Youth and family safeguards

- Follow the host partner's current youth-protection, guardian-consent, accessibility,
  emergency, pickup, photography, and reporting procedures.
- Do not create private adult-to-child messaging through these chess tools.
- Do not infer age, identity, intent, disability, or danger from chess style, body
  movement, or speculative automated classifications.
- Provide an offline or no-account route wherever practical.
- Use ratings and engine evaluations to discuss positions, never personal worth.
- Make participation possible without requiring a public username or social-media
  appearance.

## Creek work is real work

The phrase **Tiny Creek** may connect chess gatherings with practical watershed care,
but restoration is not a decorative club activity. No digging, channel alteration,
planting, debris removal, or work on public or private land should occur without the
landowner, responsible authority, and qualified environmental partner determining what
is lawful and ecologically appropriate.

A first chess pilot does not require a creek project. If a later partner adds one, keep
its budget, permissions, safety plan, and ecological outcomes distinct and explicit.

## Organizational path

Do not choose an entity merely because its label sounds virtuous. After the pilot,
compare:

1. **A program of an existing organization or fiscal sponsor.** Fastest path to a
   supervised charitable pilot when a suitable partner accepts the work.
2. **A nonprofit organization.** Appropriate only if the educational mission,
   independent governance, compliance work, fundraising plan, and public benefit
   justify maintaining a separate entity.
3. **A mission-led business or cooperative.** May support earned revenue and community
   ownership, but must state clearly when purchases are not charitable donations.
4. **A documented hybrid relationship.** Commercial work may subsidize public-benefit
   work, provided contracts, licenses, conflicts, costs, and control are transparent.

Open access, source licensing, nonprofit status, tax exemption, and free-of-charge
services are separate questions. Resolve each explicitly with qualified local legal and
tax guidance before collecting charitable funds or making organizational claims.

## Independence and attribution

Lichess provides an important example of accessible, community-supported online chess.
Chess.com, GothamChess, and Chessly provide examples of commercial chess products,
instruction, media, and audience development. Studying those models does not create a
partnership, sponsorship, endorsement, opposition campaign, or permission to use their
trademarks as this program's identity.

This pilot remains independent unless a real organization knowingly agrees otherwise
in writing.

## The decision after eight meetings

Publish a short pilot report containing the scope, aggregate participation, chess
outcomes, equipment results, complete cost ledger, difficulties, and partner feedback.
Then make one of three honest decisions:

- **repeat** the same bounded model;
- **revise** a specific weakness and run one more pilot; or
- **stop** because the evidence does not justify expansion.

Scale is not the first success condition. A complete set, a welcoming table, a useful
lesson, and a child who wants to play again are enough evidence to take the next step.
