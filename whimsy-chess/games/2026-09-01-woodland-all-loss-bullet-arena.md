# The Woodland All-Loss Bullet Expedition

> **Future revision seed:** *The Windy City Journey*. The existing review is
> preserved below; this is only a possible title or story frame for a later pass.

Five games. Five losses. Zero time forfeits. Two opponents rated above 1700. One
extremely busy Woodland emergency department.

The scoreboard says **0–5**. Ella Elouise 🦉 amended it with a smaller line
underneath:

> **Five completed experiments. Several identifiable lessons. No woodland
> creatures permanently harmed.**

## Game 1 — The Queen Steps into the Foxglove

**wquispeg 1–0 mannyfresher · Lichess `t2wIK1gV`**

The Woodland Agents began with `1...g6`, and Georgiana Gina 🦎 opened a little
path on the kingside. Harriet Hissindorf 🦔 followed with `...h6`, while Frank
Fassenbecher 🐸 bounded forward with `...f5`. It was an adventurous formation—but
the center was already opening while most of the Woodland officers remained at home
drinking acorn tea.

There was real counterplay. Dorothy Dryers 🦌 pushed through with `...d4`, Queen
Dilorias 🐲 became active on e4, and Harriet later marched all the way to h3. The
position was unusual, complicated, and very much alive. Unfortunately, after
`16.Re1 Ng4 17.f3`, Dilorias was attacked. `17...h5` continued Harriet's expedition,
but it did not answer the immediate queen problem. White played `18.fxe4`, and the
dragon vanished into the underbrush.

The encouraging feature is that Manny was trying to create threats rather than
merely waiting. The improvement is wonderfully specific: whenever a pawn attacks the
queen, move the queen before advancing another pawn. Harriet filed no objection. She
merely requested that future heroic hedgehog missions receive proper air support.

## Game 2 — King Ethelheim's Very Long Morning Walk

**mannyfresher 0–1 Rabanal · Lichess `iA8VDxll`**

This was the enormous rating mismatch: **1054 versus 1768**. Bartholomew Bogerson 🦡
opened with `1.b3`, but Dorothy 🦌 and Frank 🐸 soon joined him with `d3`, `f3`,
and then `f4`. The Woodland pawns were certainly getting exercise. Meanwhile,
several pieces remained indoors, watching the pawn parade through the window.

The tactical turning point came after Black's queen reached e4. Popette Christiana
Carolina 🛕 played `14.Bg5`, developing with apparent purpose—but the diagonal from
e4 to h1 was open. Black's `14...Qxh1+` captured Castle Hessenbach 🧱. The same
queen then collected Queen Dilorias on d1 and Castle Andora 🏰 on a1. That was less
a fork than a guided tour of every important Woodland building.

Still, King Ethelheim 🦁 refused to surrender quietly. He walked through f2, f3,
f4, back through f3 and f2, then toward e1. Against a player more than 700 points
stronger, Manny kept finding legal resistance until move 24. Ella's lesson is simple:
after the opponent moves a queen, trace every line extending from it—especially lines
ending at a rook or king. Ethelheim recommends conducting this inspection *before*
beginning the royal walking tour.

## Game 3 — The Dragon and the Newly Opened Window

**iiijasonhulk 1–0 mannyfresher · Lichess `oj3hQRGd`**

The Scandinavian began sensibly enough with `1.e4 d5`. Queen Dilorias 🐲 recaptured
on d5 and delivered `...Qe5+`, while an opposing bishop pinned the knight. The board
was sharp, tactical, and suitable for bullet—provided every exposed piece received
immediate attention.

After `5...Bxf3 6.gxf3`, White's g-pawn left its original file and the bishop on c1
gained a newly opened diagonal. Dorothy then moved from d2 to d4, clearing another
square. When Dilorias landed on g5, that bishop suddenly had a straight flight to
her. White played `8.Bxg5`, and the Woodland dragon was captured by a bishop that had
appeared inactive only moments earlier.

Castle Andora 🏰 responded energetically with `...Rd8` and `...Rxd4`, so the Woodland
side did not collapse emotionally after losing the queen. The game continued another
fifteen moves, and King Ethelheim kept moving until White finally assembled mate.
This is valuable defensive practice: the queen was lost, but the opponent still had
to finish correctly.

The pocket-sized lesson is: after any pawn moves, ask what bishop or rook it just
uncovered. Doors in chess open both ways. The Woodland maintenance crew installed a
small sign on g5 reading: **CHECK DIAGONALS BEFORE PARKING DRAGONS.**

## Game 4 — The Hedgehog Charge and the Thirty-Five-Move Afterparty

**mannyfresher 0–1 OiRichards · Lichess `sQbJzSF4`**

This was the other giant pairing: **1058 versus 1739**. The opening featured `a3`,
`c3`, `e3`, `g3`, `f4`, `d4`, `b4`, and `h4`. Nearly every Woodland pawn received
an individual launch ceremony. Sir Banyan Blithers 🦄, Dame Gertrude Goethe 🐎,
and the bishops waited patiently for their invitations.

Then Queen Dilorias 🐲 charged with `9.Qh5` and `10.Qxh7+`. It looked dramatic, and
in some attacking positions that pattern really can be dangerous. Here, however, the
opposing king could safely play `10...Kxh7`. The queen sacrifice did not purchase
mate, material, or a continuing attack. Ella gently labels it not “bad courage,” but
**courage awaiting an invoice**.

What happened afterward was genuinely impressive. Despite being down a queen against
a 1700-level opponent, the Woodland Agents survived to move 35. Frank Fassenbecher 🐸
pushed to f6, Alexander Aaronson 🦊 advanced the a-pawn deep into enemy territory,
the rooks became active, and King Ethelheim personally traveled to b3, a4, and back
again. Black even promoted a pawn before delivering mate.

That persistence deserves credit. The attack failed, but the defensive effort was
stubborn and resourceful. Before sacrificing Dilorias next time, ask one question:
**What exactly happens after the king captures her?** If the answer is “Harriet looks
concerned,” keep the queen.

## Game 5 — Fifty Moves Through the Rain

**valeriyreal 1–0 mannyfresher · Lichess `7z6gIMXi`**

The final game was the longest and, in some ways, the most encouraging. The early
difficulty arrived after `1.e4 d6 2.Nf3 Qd7 3.d4 e5 4.Bd3 c5 5.dxe5 Be7`.
White's pawn continued with `6.exd6`, then captured Pope Francisco Finochitti 🧙 on
e7. That pawn had become a dangerous visitor deep inside the Woodland camp.

Queen Dilorias 🐲 responded by roaming through g4, g2, h3, h1, e4, and d4. She
found pawns and checks, but the enemy's advanced e-pawn and lead in development
remained more urgent than the snacks along the trail. Eventually the queens were
exchanged, which gave King Ethelheim some breathing room and transformed the game
into a long rook-and-pawn struggle.

Castle Hessenbach 🧱 became quite active, capturing on f2 and moving across the sixth
rank. Even after the position simplified, Manny kept posing practical questions. The
game lasted **50 moves**. White eventually promoted the h-pawn, but the Woodland
Agents had forced an opponent rated nearly 400 points higher to demonstrate the
entire conversion.

This was not a miniature collapse. It was a difficult opening followed by a serious
defensive campaign. The next improvement is to prioritize dangerous advanced pawns
over distant pawn hunting. When an enemy pawn reaches the seventh rank, every
Woodland alarm bell should ring—even the decorative bell Bartholomew insists is
“mostly for ambiance.”

## The Woodland Council's Verdict

The recurring issue was not a lack of imagination. There was plenty. It was
**sequence**:

1. Develop the Woodland cast.
2. Check king safety.
3. Check whether Queen Dilorias is attacked.
4. Then unleash the strange forest machinery.

For the next bullet tournament, Ella proposes one compact rule:

> During the first six moves, develop at least two pieces. Before every queen move,
> check the destination square's rows, files, and diagonals.

Confidence is welcome in the clearing. It gives Harriet permission to explore and
Dilorias permission to attack. The modest safeguard is not a sermon against feeling
good: take one breath before the leap and ask what the opponent can capture. Confidence
and checking can travel together.

Harriet 🦔 calls the tournament “five scouting missions.” Dilorias 🐲 requested
improved flight clearance. King Ethelheim 🦁 would prefer fewer emergency hikes.
