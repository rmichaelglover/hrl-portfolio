# Woodland Watershed Atlas — evidence ledger

Status: **research record, not yet story canon**. Updated 2026-08-03.

This ledger separates measured public geography from fictional names and private
household details. Exact residential addresses and coordinates are intentionally absent
from the public record. “St. James Creek,” “Mr. Harrison's Creek,” “Lake Michael,” and
the numbered Tiny Creeks are local story names until matched to survey, parcel,
stormwater, or field evidence.

## Verified regional anchors

- The Skyland Trail adult main-campus area and the nearby West Druid Hills Drive
  behavioral-health area both snap to **NHDPlus COMID 2047971** in the USGS Network
  Linked Data Index. The mapped flowline runs generally west and southwest into the
  North Fork Peachtree Creek system.
- USGS 3DEP one-meter samples at the public address points are approximately **881 ft**
  and **841 ft** above the service datum, respectively. These are address-point samples,
  not campus minima or maxima.
- The generalized southwest-Marietta home area and nearby Arden Drive reference both
  snap to **NHDPlus COMID 3285556**. The national flowline runs generally south and
  southwest through the Sweetwater Creek/Chattahoochee drainage network.
- The corresponding generalized address-point samples are approximately **1,026 ft**
  and **995 ft**. They do **not** establish the highest or lowest point of either parcel.
- Surface water from both verified networks ultimately enters the Chattahoochee; the
  Chattahoochee joins the Apalachicola system and reaches the Gulf of Mexico. “All
  oceans, continents, and Gaia” remains poetic scale, not literal downstream topology.

## Provisional flow stories

The following may be used only with words such as *proposed*, *locally named*, or
*awaiting verification*:

1. backyard runoff → locally named St. James Creek → locally named Mr. Harrison's
   Creek → Olley Creek;
2. Olley Creek → Sweetwater Creek → Chattahoochee River;
3. numbered Tiny Creeks → locally named Lake Michael;
4. the Northeast Atlanta pond/creek complex → North Fork Peachtree Creek.

The national datasets support the regional receiving networks, not every backyard
connection. Culverts and storm pipes can cross a surface-derived divide, so elevation
alone cannot prove the micro-route.
