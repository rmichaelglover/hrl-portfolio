    A GEOGRAPHY VARIANT, WITH CLASSICAL CHESS FRIENDS

    Good rules help everyone flourish.

    🧭 Learn the roles. Figurines tell you what a friend can do. Straight and diagonal routes follow the map; knights jump two steps, then one sideways.

    🤝 Ask, listen, agree. Practice starts with everyone cooperating. A council game takes turns and allows up to three alliance offers per rival. If every active council agrees, everyone shares the win.

    🫶 Everyone stays safe. Taken pieces rest on the friendship bench. Checkmate finishes a chess lesson; it does not harm its characters. Kings and queens begin every council together.

    For grown-ups & curious learners: geography and chess rules

    This is an experimental geographic chess variant. Provinces have irregular borders and approximate compass routes, not ordinary square-board geometry. Pawns move one mapped step forward, rather than having standard chess’s initial two-step option. Castling and en passant are not included. Antarctic and northern polar spaces allow promotion; approaching another shore can also promote a traveling pawn.

    Each of the three councils starts with exactly sixteen pieces in sixteen different places. Small countries use recorded Natural Earth city points; when fewer than sixteen real province/city spaces are in this atlas, additional spaces are clearly labeled imaginary story gardens. These gardens are not real cities or new political boundaries. Piece emojis are wildlife-inspired story ambassadors, and can travel beyond an animal’s natural range.

    Local shared-screen play only. An adult can help younger learners choose teams and take turns. Countries, territories, and disputed map areas all appear; this is a playful atlas, not a political endorsement. Research: All Roads Lead to Home ↗ · Rules, sources, and design notes ↗

      🌿 Field notes: where these neighbors belong

      This is an original, fictional friendship world. Named wildlife uses broad country and geographic-range sketches, coastal hints, and rough habitat filters. These are illustrative distributions, not verified sightings, abundance estimates, or exact habitat maps. Unresearched regions use explicitly labeled animal groups; some quiet places have no character.

      Night brings different neighbors. Koalas belong to the eastern mainland Australia sketch; kiwi neighbors stay in the main New Zealand sketch; penguins use southern coastal anchors. Invited friends are story visitors, separate from native map residents. Your chosen avatar is a traveler, not a distribution claim.

      Geography: Natural Earth v5.1.2. Countries, territories, and mapped disputed areas share this world; map coverage is not a sovereignty count. Gardens and gathering materials are imagined. Research: All Roads Lead to Home ↗ · Model, sources, and design notes ↗
